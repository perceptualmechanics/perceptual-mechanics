## perceptualmechanics.com — Quiz

The fourteenth scene. Working name: **Quiz**.

And it is the right name for the same reason Medium was. Every other scene name
reaches — Apollo, Psyshell, Orrery, Beamline. *Quiz* reaches for nothing, which
is exactly the register, because the whole mechanism depends on nothing
signalling what is coming. A visitor sees *Quiz* in the nav and knows what a
quiz is.

And then it isn't one. Same move as the Night Market's dingy fluorescent and
the Bottle's sealed container: the plainest possible label, telling you exactly
what it is, right up until it turns out to be more.

It started as a bit and stopped being one. Filed as a draft to look at rather
than a spec to build.

### What it is

A sincere personality inventory. Anodyne questions, real scoring — and the
result is Yeats, verbatim, at full volume.

The visitor answers fifteen or so ordinary items on a five-point scale. Nothing
signals what is coming. Behind them, two gyres turn, which reads as background
imagery. At the end, the gyres stop being decorative and become the answer, and
the report arrives in the language of *A Vision*: the phase, the True and False
Masks, the Body of Fate, the characteristic failure stated as cosmic law.

### Why it is not satire

Because Yeats meant it. The scene does not exaggerate him — it declines to
soften him.

The comedy is entirely in the tonal gap, and nobody sets it up. The reader has
been answering *I prefer to finish one thing before starting another* and gets
back a sentence about the antithetical tincture and the Daimon.

Two rules protect it:

- **No self-awareness anywhere.** The moment the interface acknowledges the joke
  it becomes a parody. It should look like a real instrument built by someone
  with no sense of humour.
- **The items must be answerable honestly.** If they are absurd, it is a joke.
  If a person genuinely thinks about them, the arrival of Phase 17 lands as an
  accusation.

Which is the setting's actual claim delivered as a bit: an elaborate system,
sincerely applied, producing an authoritative answer nobody can check.

### Why it belongs in this project rather than being a good gag

Sandover states that Yeats is living in DJ's hand throughout — he is
effectively the third author.

So the lineage is a chain of received systems, each received through the
previous one's method. Yeats gets *A Vision* from George's automatic writing,
four days into their marriage. Merrill and Jackson get *Sandover* from a cup,
with Yeats in the hand doing the moving. And **Medium** (the thirteenth scene)
is a board where the second party is real and neither sitter can perceive their
own contribution.

Which puts the two scenes in a genuine relationship rather than a thematic one.
Medium is the apparatus. This is what a system produced by that apparatus looks
like when you are on the receiving end — sincere, elaborate, authoritative,
unverifiable.

And it is the fourth link in that chain. A system received through a channel
nobody controls, applied to you, with total confidence. The site is the medium
this time.

### Settled: it is a genuine quiz, and the mapping is honest

Deterministic. Same answers, same phase, every time. No séance, no drift, no
randomisation.

This is a personality-quiz interface on top of one of the weirdest systems ever
devised, played entirely straight. The instrument is real, the scoring is real,
and the mapping to Yeats' structure is honest rather than decorative. Nothing is
faked to make the joke work, because the joke is that nothing needs to be.

### The instrument — how it actually scores

Yeats' Faculties are already stated as pairs of opposites, which is what a
psychometric scale is.

> "Will and Mask are the will and its object, or the Is and the Ought; Creative
> Mind and Body of Fate are thought and its object, or the Knower and the Known.
> The first two are lunar or antithetical; the second two solar or primary."

Two scales, not four, which is a cleaner instrument and is Yeats' own structure:

**Scale 1 — Will ↔ Mask.** Is versus Ought. Do you act from what you are, or
toward what you want to become? *I would rather be good at what I already do
than start something I would be bad at. When I imagine a better version of
myself, it is recognisably me.*

**Scale 2 — Creative Mind ↔ Body of Fate.** Knower versus Known. Do you
understand a situation by thinking about it, or by what it does to you? *I
usually know why something upset me. Things happen to me that I could not have
predicted.*

Scoring is summation, never branching. Six to eight items per scale, some
reverse-keyed, every item adding to a dimension rather than sending the visitor
down a path. Which is what makes it impossible to see coming — no single answer
feels decisive.

### The elegant part

Will's position determines everything else. Yeats says phases take their names
and numbers from where Will sits: Mask goes opposite, Creative Mind and Body of
Fate at their determined points.

So one composite score places one Faculty and the other three follow by
construction — and the report can then state all four positions as though it
measured them.

Which is exactly the unearned authority the joke needs, and it is Yeats' own
arithmetic rather than a cheat.

### The two poles

Phases 1 and 15 are uninhabitable — complete objectivity and complete beauty, no
human incarnation at either. The instrument must never return them. And the
report should say why, in Yeats' terms, which is funnier and truer than silently
excluding them.

### The source: yeatsvision.com

Neil Mann's site is the resource — he is one of the editors of *Yeats's "A
Vision": Explications and Contexts* (Clemson/Liverpool UP), so this is
scholarship rather than an enthusiast page, and everything is cited to AV A and
AV B page numbers.

Structure: a page per phase (`/Ph1.html`–`/Ph28.html`), plus Quarters, Tinctures,
Faculties, Principles, Geometry, Terminology, Twelvefold, Wheel.

### And it hands you the report's architecture

The wheel divides into four Quarters, each with a dominant Faculty and an
element — the Fourth is Fire, Body of Fate dominant, "the quarter of
completion." Phases also group into triads, and there are Cardinal Phases.

So a report can state, honestly and from the system: your phase · your quarter ·
its element · its dominant Faculty · your triad · and the positions of all four
Faculties.

Which is a Golden Dawn report already. That is exactly the shape of a GD grade
attribution — element, correspondence, the whole apparatus stacked.

### The register comes free

Yeats' own prose, via the site's citations:

> "standing in the presence of a terrible blinding light" · "Hunchback and Saint
> and Fool are the last crescents" · "Man does not perceive the truth; God
> perceives the truth in man"

No invention required. The portentousness is the source material.

### And Yeats names real people per phase

Rembrandt and Synge at 23. Victoria, Lady Gregory and Galsworthy at 24. Luther,
Calvin, Newman at 25. Socrates and Pascal at 27.

A report that tells you which historical figures share your phase is doing the
personality-quiz move and the Yeats move at the same time — and it is his list,
not an invention.

### The report — Golden Dawn, biblical angels

The tonal gap is the whole scene, so the report is the piece that has to land.

Not a paragraph. A full apparatus. Element, quarter, dominant Faculty, the four
Faculty positions, the triad, the True and False Masks, the Body of Fate, the
characteristic failure — stacked, formal, and stated as fact.

The reference is a Golden Dawn grade report and biblical angels: portentous,
structural, and completely without hedging. Not ominous — certain.

And the visitor arrived here by answering *I prefer to finish one thing before
starting another*.

### Copyright — the rule, settled

**Facts and structure are free. Prose is not.**

Take the wheel, take the names, take the attributions, write the sentences.

Not copyrightable, and usable directly: the twenty-eight phases; the quarters
and their elements; which Faculty dominates where; the triads and Cardinal
Phases; and Yeats' own attributions of historical figures to phases. All
structure and fact — state every bit of it in your own words.

Protected: his sentences. "Standing in the presence of a terrible blinding
light" is a quote — it gets quotation marks and a citation, or it does not
appear.

So the phase descriptions get written fresh, in the register, from the
structure. More work, and better: a paraphrase of Yeats reads like a paraphrase.
Something written to be portentous in a Golden Dawn report reads like a report.
The source gives the skeleton and the tone; the words are ours.

Mann's site is the same deal one layer up — his organisation and glosses are
his, his citations point at Yeats. A research source, not a text to lift.

### The visual

Two interpenetrating gyres, turning, as background. Each cone's base at the
other's apex, one widening as the other narrows — so any point is near-maximum
on one and near-minimum on the other. Both-at-once as an object.

It reads as ambient while the quiz runs, and becomes the answer at the end.
Whatever the reveal is, the gyres were already showing it.

This is the risk in the scene and it is a looking question, not a reasoning one.
Conceptually the gyres are beautiful; on screen they may be two cones. The
branch and the blossom both died here. Render early, look, and report before
building the rest around it.

### Open, and genuinely open

- How many items? Six to eight per scale is the working figure; the exact count
  is a tuning question once the items exist.
- What does the report look like? The tonal gap is the whole scene, so this is
  the piece that has to land.

### Scene requirements

Read `SITE.md`, `STANDARDS.md`, `CORRECTED-FACTS.md`, and both audit punch lists
first. Source: https://www.yeatsvision.com — Neil Mann.

- `src/scenes/quiz/quiz.{js,css,html}` plus `quiz.text.js` carrying the items
  and the phase table.
- A `/text/` page is required and is unusually interesting here — the phase
  descriptions are the content, and the crawlable form is the honest place for
  them.
- This is a form, so the accessibility bar is higher than a canvas scene's: real
  inputs, real labels, a fieldset per scale, keyboard-completable start to
  finish, and a result announced to `sr-live`.
- The landing requirement: no icons below the fold on desktop. Thirteen
  currently fits in two rows. Fourteen has to be shown to fit before this ships.
- The nav is scrollable as of 5.x — confirm the fourteenth icon rather than
  assume.
- State must not persist (`STANDARDS.md`). A visitor who returns gets a fresh
  instrument, not their old phase.

## Answered during the build

- **Item count: eight per scale**, sixteen in all. Seven would split the keying
  four and three and leave an acquiescence drift in the result.
- **The report is a verdict, not a summary.** Scott, mid-build: *"once the quiz
  is done, it disappears and the gyres WHIRL INTO FULL FRAME AND THEN THE
  JUDGMENT ACCORDING TO THE QUIZ IS DISPENSED. IT SHOULD FEEL VERY ALL CAPS AND
  IN YOUR FACE AND A TOTAL TONAL CONTRAST TO WHAT JUST CAME BEFORE. THIS IS YOUR
  DESTINY AS DETERMINED BY THE CYCLE."* And: *"BE AS DETAILED AS POSSIBLE, AS
  MUCH AS YEATS ALLOWED. TREAT EVERYTHING IN THE SYSTEM AS LITERAL TRUTH."*
- **A scrim behind the text**, rather than a text colour chosen to survive a
  spiral crossing it, because no single colour does.
- **The single gyre behind the form is not a tell worth fixing.** Scott: *"if
  someone gets it, kudos to them. They're a Yeats scholar and a weirdo."*
- **The landing tile draws a questionnaire, not the wheel.** A tile of turning
  gyres promises mystery, which is the one thing the scene must not promise.

## Where the build broke the brief, and how

Recorded because the pattern matters more than the list.

**Nothing signals what is coming**, broken in four places at once by 6.1.0 and
fixed in 6.1.1: a preamble naming Yeats, the phases and the Wheel; items written
in this site's voice rather than a questionnaire's; `registry.js` repeating the
whole premise into the landing tile's aria-label; and a rim of twenty-eight
marks turning behind the questions.

**The two scales**, broken by 6.1.0 and fixed in 6.2.0. The brief names them —
Will ↔ Mask, Creative Mind ↔ Body of Fate — and the build measured
antithetical-against-primary and waxing-against-waning instead. Defensible axes,
invented here, and not the ones the system supplies. The whole claim the scene
makes is that the mapping is honest, so an invented pair of axes is not a detail.

**The triad**, missing from the report until 6.2.0, and Cowork had additionally
told Scott the brief was wrong about triads existing. It was not: the Faculty
rectangles and the Triads are two different structures and both are Yeats's
(AV B 92-93).

**The symbols**, quoted verbatim from an edited scholarly edition until 6.2.0,
which is the one place the copyright rule bites in this scene. Restated now.
