Hywel Bowen

Human · Acolyte · Cleric 6 (Hidden) · level 6
ShowcaseLevel 6Hidden DomainThe Welsh border, 1668

Every border village has someone who goes between: the fairy doctor in Clare, the n'anga at the pool, the farmer's wife at the lava rock. In Morfudd Prys's hill village above the old mines, it's the parson. The Killer stands at the gate with iron; he stands in the church tower with a bell, and in the lane at dusk with bread, asking for things back.

The church bell has been going since sunset, and the man ringing it is nearly sixty and out of breath. Between peals he leans out of the tower and calls down into the mist in Welsh, politely, the way you'd speak to a neighbor who has your cow: a child went missing at the ford, and he would like to talk about what would be fair.

Human

Human (2024): Resourceful (Heroic Inspiration after each Long Rest); Skillful (one skill: History); Versatile (an origin feat: Healer). Not a Hidden creature, and nothing in him remembers being one: the Hidden Domain speaks for the village and carries no piece of the Hidden. The good people know him on sight anyway.

Gear

A threadbare black gown and bands; a pewter cross on a cord as his Holy Symbol; Y Beibl Bach, the little Welsh Bible of 1630, its margins full of names and dates; a Healer's Kit; the key to the bell tower; a pocket full of bread at all hours; a walking staff of rowan (as a quarterstaff, +2, 1d6 − 1); a lantern. Languages: Welsh (in place of Common), English, Latin, and Sylvan (The Go-Between). Tools: Calligrapher's Supplies (Acolyte): the ledger, the parish register, and the occasional bargain written out fair.

Adder Stone, Uncommon (attuned). A stone with a hole worn clean through it by water. Look through the hole and you see the good people as they are. Held to your eye (a Bonus Action to raise, and it keeps that hand busy): you see Hidden creatures within 60 ft in their true forms through any illusion, glamour or shape-change, and see Invisible ones there. A Hidden Domain Cleric who carries it has Advantage on the first Charisma (Persuasion) check of each bargain.

Iron and Salt (Adventuring Gear, cheap and common). A crock of salt in the vestry and a box of horseshoe nails under the pulpit. Iron fixed at a door, window or chimney, with a line of salt across it, wards that opening until the next dawn. Ten minutes wards every opening of a room or small building (a great house: 10 minutes a floor or wing), and while every opening is warded a Hidden creature can't enter the place by any means, teleportation included, unless a creature inside that isn't Hidden asks it in aloud (freely, for pay, by a lie or Charmed: it counts; it admits the one asked and its company, until dawn or until taken back; a lodger's can last the stay). No save crosses a ward. An unwarned householder who hears a voice at the door makes a Wisdom save against the asker's spell save DC (DC 13 if the asker has none; Advantage while the Hidden are at the ebb) and asks it in on a failure; one told plainly what is asking succeeds unless Charmed. Moving the iron or breaking the salt ends the ward on that opening (a Utilize action for anyone who isn't Hidden; no Hidden creature can do it by hand, by a thrown object or by magic), and a new hole has no ward until someone gives it one. The church door has had a horseshoe over it since before the Reformation. Two bishops have asked him to take it down.

The ledger. A calf-bound account book, in Welsh, of every bargain he has struck with the Hidden for the parish: a column for who or what was asked for (a child, a cow, the Pritchards' butter), one for the price, one for what's paid. The third column is short. When the Hidden owe him, he calls the debt in once, and they fail their saves against his Turn Aside until they pay.

Background

Hywel Bowen was put into the living at the Restoration and expected a quiet parish. He got one where the Cŵn Annwn run the lane in the dark half and the Killer is a dwarf, and he adapted. He rings the church bell on the open nights; Morfudd rings hers at the gate; between them the village has lost fewer than its neighbors. His bishop thinks he preaches against superstition, and he does, on Sundays. On the other six days he keeps a ledger of what the parish owes the good people and what they owe it, and he is behind on the payments. He has spoken once to Wat Ellery, the Hunt's boy, across a ford, and hasn't told Morfudd what was said. What he wants is to bring the last Killer home before he's too old to climb the tower.

How to play

  • Talk first. Parley before Initiative, and a fight you never have is the best fight a go-between has. You use Wisdom, with Advantage, to bargain with the Hidden.
  • Know the tide before you open your mouth. At the flood, Turn Aside is strong and asking for anything back is dear; at the ebb, Bring Back heals double and the Hidden can't keep hold of what they took.
  • Tie your Rowan and Red Thread on whoever the Hunt will go for, on every rest: Advantage against their charms, and one fall to 0 that becomes 1.
  • Bring Back calls home anyone Taken within 30 ft, whatever took them (a Mortal Hunter, a Dreamlord), unless the Hunt drew you onto thin ground. Keep the fight off the fairy ring and the ford.
  • Out of a fight, the ledger is the adventure: every bargain has a price, and every unpaid one is a debt the Hidden can call in, or that you can.

Why this build

  • Level 6, as the roster has him: Rowan and Red Thread, the ward he ties on the parish children before the dark half. A country parson, not a famous cunning man.
  • Acolyte (Wis +2, Cha +1; Magic Initiate (Cleric)): a priest by training, whatever the Hidden chose him for. Healer (human Versatile): a parson visits the sick. War Caster at 4th (+1 Wis): Wisdom 18, and Spirit Guardians that holds while he rings.
  • Thaumaturge and Potent Spellcasting, no armor: a man near sixty in a gown. AC 11 is why he talks first.
  • The Adder Stone: the Welsh call it glain neidr, the snake's glass; held to his eye in the lane, it shows him what the mist is wearing.
