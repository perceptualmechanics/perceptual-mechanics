Akinwale

Human · Soldier · Paladin 7 (Glory) · level 7
On the coverOyo, 1668The Yoruba world

In Euphonia the Yoruba are West Africa's great Wisdom power, and in the field that power looks like spirit riding: the orisha mount their devotees. When Oyo's cavalry rides, some of its riders are ridden. He is one of them. Who rides him is between him and his orisha, and his sheet doesn't name it; the rite isn't depicted. Whether the power gathers at Ife, at Oyo, or at both is each table's to decide.

The Nupe line is set on the far bank, spears grounded, and Oyo's horsemen come down to the ford at a walk. Halfway across, one of them stops riding like a man. His back straightens, his face is somebody else's, and the horse, which knows, goes from a walk to a gallop without being asked. The men on the bank remember afterward that he was laughing. He doesn't remember any of it.

Background

Akinwale ("valor came home") was born into a family of horsemen in the metropolitan towns around Oyo Ile, when the court was newly home from its long exile, and his family came back with it; his grandfather rode with Abipa's generation and told him what exile was. He was given to his orisha young, as his mother had been, and the first time he was mounted was at a festival, in front of the whole quarter; the second time was on a raid into Borgu, in front of the enemy. The war chiefs noticed. Now he rides in the front rank, where a ridden man is worth ten, and he keeps the observances of his orisha with the care of someone who knows exactly what he is carrying. The horse cost more than his house. It came from Nupe, the people he fights, because Oyo can't breed enough of its own, and keeping it alive at the edge of the forest is half his work.

How to play

  • Spend a Channel Divinity before the charge: being ridden is an hour of Peerless Athlete, and the second use is Inspiring Smite on the hit that matters.
  • Fight from the saddle. Mounted Combatant gives Advantage against foot soldiers and keeps the horse alive; Topple puts them on the ground for the riders behind you.
  • Aura of Alacrity is a cavalry aura: every ally who comes within 10 ft of you moves 10 ft further. Ride at the front and let the line catch up.
  • Play the riding with respect. The orisha is not you, and not a power-up: the player and the DM decide together what the rider remembers, and what the orisha asks of him afterward.
  • Out of a fight, you are a horseman at the tsetse line: Animal Handling, Lesser Restoration and Lay on Hands keep a horse alive where the forest kills them.

Why this build

  • Paladin: of the classes that could carry possession in battle, the Paladin is the one whose power is devotion kept in a body, and the one built to fight. His Channel Divinity is his being ridden. A Cleric of his orisha is the other fit, for a rider who is a priest first.
  • Oath of Glory: a power of the body, beyond what the body can do (Peerless Athlete), shared with the people beside him (Inspiring Smite, Aura of Alacrity). The Knight of Malta on the cover keeps Devotion.
  • Level 7: a proven front-rank rider, not a war chief. It brings Extra Attack, Find Steed and the Aura of Alacrity.
  • Soldier (Str +2, Con +1; Savage Attacker), Alert (human Versatile) for the first charge, Mounted Combatant at 4th: a cavalryman's feats. Dueling with a lance held in one hand: +7, 1d10 + 6.
