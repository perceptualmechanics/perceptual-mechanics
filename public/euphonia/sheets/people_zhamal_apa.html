On the Kazakh steppe the albasty is a spirit that attacks women in childbirth. Midwives drive it off with a whip at the threshold, untied knots and a loud announcement that a hero has arrived. The Physician's Hedge Witch, who heals by taking, is the class that fits her.

A yurt at night, a woman in labor, every knot in the tent untied and a whip laid across the threshold. An old midwife goes to the door and shouts "The hero has come!" with such conviction that the hero, Aisulu, a head taller than the door, ducks in looking embarrassed. Something at the smoke-hole leaves in a hurry.

Background

A Kazakh midwife of the same camp as Aisulu and Kenzhe, a Hedge Witch who drives the albasty off birthing mothers by announcing that a hero has arrived, and pays for every life in her own pain. The lamas call it superstition; she says the lamas have never had a baby. She wants a girl to train who won't flinch, and not to be sent for in winter.

How to play

  • She shouts, she whips the threshold, and she takes the pain. Play her loud.
  • Communal Pain is the whole of her art: she pays for every life in her own pain.
  • The lamas think she is superstitious. Let her be right.
  • The hook is the girl who won't flinch. A player character might be her.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: forty years of births, and Communal Pain.
  • Hedge Witch, as the roster has her: healing that costs.
  • Cunning Folk (Euphonia.md) and Healer for a camp midwife.

Physician (Hedge Witch)

All back on a Long Rest, 2 on a Short Rest.
1Diagnosis. Bonus Action, one creature you can see within 30 ft (you can choose yourself, as willing): willing, or an ally that can't refuse (Incapacitated or at 0 Hit Points), automatic (current and maximum Hit Points, every condition, disease, Poisoned, or curse, and its lowest save); otherwise Intelligence (Medicine) vs DC 10 + ½ CR (10 + ½ level without a CR): unharmed, injured, Bloodied, or near death; conditions, disease, poison, curse; Necrotic and Poison Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability and disease Immunity; its lowest save (the stat block's, or the ability modifier if none is listed; on a tie the DM names either). A failure: not again on it until a rest. Everyone you diagnose is your patient until a Long Rest, any number at once; healing a patient with a Physician feature or spell rerolls 1s on the healing dice.
2Treatment. Utilize action, one use of a Healer's Kit, your patient within reach: ending Poisoned needs no check (unless an effect with a save DC imposed it: then that DC); otherwise Intelligence (Medicine) vs Blinded or Deafened 12, a disease 13 (beaten the slow way: never caught again), Paralyzed 15, a level of Exhaustion 15 (once per creature per Long Rest). A success ends the effect, and any spell imposing it on the creature; either way the kit use is spent, and not the same treatment on that creature again until it rests.
3Rip Life. Healing Prowess as a rip: a creature within 30 ft that isn't a Construct or Undead makes a Con save (DC 15) or takes 1d8+4 Necrotic, half on a success; either way, you or a creature within 30 ft regains the number rolled+4.
3Cunning Folk. 5To the Wounded. On your turn, while you move toward a creature that isn't hostile to you and is Bloodied or at 0 Hit Points (every foot bringing you closer), your Speed increases by 10 ft and your movement doesn't provoke Opportunity Attacks. You've crossed worse floors than this to reach a patient.

Damage hostile creatures deal you, including what you take for others, is stored as Pain (up to 3 × level), lost when the aura ends; Magic action to release all of it: Retribution (Necrotic to a creature within 30 ft, Con save DC 15 for half) or Restitution (healing to a creature within 30 ft).
7Vitals. When you use Healing Prowess, Diagnosis on a different creature as part of it (the same Bonus Action, or attack). Advantage on saves against disease and the Poisoned condition.
