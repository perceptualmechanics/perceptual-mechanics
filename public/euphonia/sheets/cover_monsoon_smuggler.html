Daeng Calla

Halfling · Criminal · Rogue 7 (Thief) · level 7
On the coverMakassar, 1668The Monsoon RoadHalfling

Makassar is still fighting a year after it signed the peace: Sultan Hasanuddin signed the Treaty of Bongaya in November 1667, felt cheated, and went back to war, and the brick fortress of Somba Opu still stands. The VOC wants Makassar's free port shut. Meanwhile every court's minister of magic wants the Mutapan curiosities coming up the Monsoon Road licensed, stamped, and counted. She moves them without the stamp.

The Dutch boarding officer has the manifest in one hand and a lantern in the other, and the lantern is the only light in the hold, because she switched off the other one with her heel when she heard him on the ladder. He asks what is in the long crate. Rattan, she tells him, and smiles, and the crate behind her, which is full of lamps that light themselves, is very quiet.

Every grandmother in the archipelago tells of the orang bunian, the hidden people who live beside ours unseen; hers says the family came from them, crossed over in a halfling's shape, and forgot. Mutapan Curiosities (Adventuring Gear; electrical, not magical). Her own: a self-lighting lamp (Bright Light 20 ft and Dim Light 20 ft more, no flame, on or off with a touch), which is how she reads charts below decks with the hatches shut. The hold: a crate of twelve, lamps, cold boxes and two clockwork songbirds, bought at Sofala's price and bound for buyers who don't want a ministry stamp on them. She has nobody a Mhizha taught, which is the weak point of the business. Abroad they sell at five times Sofala's price in a Monsoon Road port and ten in Europe.

Background

Daeng Calla is a Makassarese halfling, born in the free port when it was the place every ship that wouldn't take a Dutch pass came to trade: Portuguese, English, Danes, Malays, Chinese, all welcome. Her father had a share in a padewakang and she had the rest of him: the charts, the debts, and the friends in every harbor from Banten to Ternate. When the curiosities started coming up the Monsoon Road she was among the first to understand that a lamp that lights itself is worth more in Batavia's back rooms than a hold of cloves, and that a stamp is a tax. Now the Dutch and Arung Palakka's Bugis are under Somba Opu's walls, and her harbor is a war. She still sails. Somebody has to get messages out through the lines, and somebody has to pay for powder, and she can do both in one voyage.

How to play

  • You are not a fighter, and you don't have to be. Most of your fights are a conversation with a customs officer, and your Deception is +8.
  • You are small, and hiding is your great strength: Naturally Stealthy lets you Hide behind the boarding officer himself, Halfling Nimbleness takes you through a crowded deck, and Luck rerolls the 1 when the customs man looks twice.
  • Fast Hands is the smuggler's feature: palming, picking, and switching off a lamp with one hand while the other gives the officer your pass.
  • When it does come to a fight, go first, strike from where they aren't looking, and leave: Sneak Attack, then Cunning Action, then gone up the rigging (Second-Story Work).
  • The curiosities drift and you can't retune them. Every voyage and every storm is a d6 for each, so find someone a Mhizha taught, or a buyer before the d6 comes up 1.
  • Somba Opu is under siege. The best-paid cargo this year isn't lamps; it's a message, or a prince, through the Bugis lines.

Why this build

  • Thief: the brief offers the Arcane Trickster; the Thief is the smuggler's subclass (Fast Hands, Second-Story Work), and keeps her magic in the cargo, where the story is. No other cover figure is a Rogue.
  • Level 7: a captain with her own ship and her own contacts, not a crime lord. Evasion and Reliable Talent arrive at 7th.
  • Criminal (Dex +2, Con +1; Alert). Halfling: hiding is the halfling's great strength, and hers is a smuggler's. Rogue skills lean to talk (Persuasion) over climbing, since Second-Story Work does the climbing.
  • Makassar: the brief gives Batavia or Makassar; a Makassarese smuggler puts the 1668 war on the cover, and Makassar was the free port the VOC was fighting to close.
