Dom Garcia Nsaku

Human · Noble · Fighter 9 (Battle Master) · level 9
On the coverSoyo, 1668The mouth of the Congo

Kongo has been Catholic for 150 years, and at Mbwila in 1665 an army from Portuguese Luanda killed its king and much of its nobility. The power rising out of the wreck is Soyo, Kongo's own coastal province at the mouth of the Congo, rich on salt and Dutch trade, which has beaten every army sent against it. Its noble shield-bearers, the adargueiros, fight with broadswords and no armor.

The royal army comes down to the edge of the sacred wood in its best order, and the shield-bearers of Soyo come out to meet it dancing: the sangamento, swords flashing, shields turning, a display of every cut they mean to make. Then the dance stops, and it turns out they meant every one.

Background

Born to a noble house of Soyo in the years when the counts were turning back royal invasions one after another; his father carried a shield in the wood of Nfinda Ngula when the king's army broke on it, and taught him the sword in the plaza at Mbanza Soyo. He reads Portuguese and his catechism, hears Mass from the Capuchins, keeps a brass crucifix, and sees no contradiction in any of it. He was not at Mbwila, and he has never decided whether he is glad. Now Count Paulo da Silva holds the king at São Salvador in his hand, and every lord in Kongo wants to know whether Soyo means to reunite the kingdom, rule it, or keep it broken. He would like to know too. He carries the count's messages to people who would rather not receive them, and comes back.

How to play

  • No armor is the point: you fight light, and your defense is the shield, Parry, Evasive Footwork and footwork of your own. AC 15 is low; your Superiority Dice make up the difference. Spend them.
  • Your dice come back on a Short Rest: two in every fight, more in the one that matters.
  • The dance is a weapon. Commanding Presence and Performance turn a parade into a threat, and a threat into a negotiation.
  • You carry a count's messages. History and Persuasion are the court; Insight is knowing which of the king's men is already Soyo's.
  • Tough and Second Wind keep you standing where a man without armor shouldn't be: 94 Hit Points.

Why this build

  • Battle Master: the trained professional, and Kongo's shield-bearers were exactly that; the maneuvers are the parries and the dance. No other cover figure is a Fighter.
  • Level 9: a veteran of Soyo's wars, not its general; Indomitable and Tactical Master at 9th.
  • Noble (Str +2, Cha +1; Skilled): the adargueiros were Kongo's nobility. Tough (human Versatile): what a man without armor needs.
  • Strength 20 by 8th; Dexterity 16 and a shield give AC 15 without armor, as the region chapter says they fought.

Maneuvers

Parry. Reaction when a creature hits you with a melee attack: reduce the damage by a Superiority Die + your Strength or Dexterity modifier. (Player's Handbook)

Riposte. Reaction when a creature misses you with a melee attack: attack it, adding a Superiority Die to the damage. (Player's Handbook)

Evasive Footwork. Bonus Action, a Superiority Die: Disengage, and add the die to your AC until the start of your next turn. (Player's Handbook)

Disarming Attack. On a hit, add a Superiority Die to the damage; the target makes a Strength save or drops an object you choose. (Player's Handbook)

Commanding Presence. Add a Superiority Die to a Charisma (Intimidation, Performance or Persuasion) check: the sangamento, the armed dance before battle. (Player's Handbook)
