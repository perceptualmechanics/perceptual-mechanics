An instrument-maker's daughter who built glassware for the Accademia del Cimento until it closed last year, when its patron took a cardinal's hat, and now writes to the Royal Society in Latin under her brother's name. She wants to find out whether a spell weighs anything.

Her father's workshop near Santa Croce. A young woman in a leather apron sets a sealed flask on a balance, adjusts the rider to a grain, writes the figure down, and turns to the visiting priest. "Bless it, please," she says. "Anything you like. Then I'll weigh it again." He hesitates. She has already dipped her pen.

The Florentine thermometer. Her keepsake: a thermometer of the Cimento pattern, spirit of wine sealed in a coiled glass stem with enamel beads for degrees, made by her own hand. It's the first thing she ever sealed that didn't crack, and the last thing the Accademia ordered.

The sealed flask. A flask of air, sealed at the blowpipe, that she has weighed after every spell anyone would cast at it. Eleven spells. The same weight, to the grain. She keeps the list.

Background

Lisabetta Mannucci learned to blow glass before she learned to read, in a workshop that made thermometers and barometers for the Medici. When the Accademia del Cimento needed vessels that wouldn't crack under a vacuum or a freezing mixture, her father's shop made them, and by the end she was making them alone and suggesting the experiments. The Accademia's motto was trying and trying again; it stopped trying in 1667. She hasn't. Her letters to London go by Genoa and are read by at least two services on the way, which she knows, so she writes the important parts as glassware orders. Her question is simple and nobody has asked it properly: if magic is real, does a spell weigh anything? So far, to the grain, no. She is not finished.

How to play

  • Two experiments a day, the energy chosen as you throw: pick it for the trouble in front of you. Lightning's Line is for soldiers in iron; Thunder pushes a crowd off a stair; Acid lowers AC until a Long Rest.
  • Overproof when it has to work: two more dice, and Volatile doubles its odds. Insulation (10th) is the day it stops hurting.
  • When the experiments are spent you're still a Natural Philosopher: Mechanism uses the room, and the pistol uses Calculated Shot.
  • Demonstration: an hour, an audience, a bell falling silent in an emptied jar, and 24 hours of Advantage on persuading anyone who watched. It's how the Cimento was paid for.
  • Touchstone: you can tell platina from silver, which almost no one outside Mutapa can. Florence's goldsmiths would pay to know that. So would Spain's forgers, to know who else knows.

Why this build

  • Level 6, as the roster has her: young, skilled, and between patrons. Overproof and Touchstone (6th) are the experimentalist and the assayer; Insulation (10th) is a long way off, which is why her scars are on the sheet.
  • Artisan (Int +2, Dex +1; Crafter): an instrument-maker's daughter before she was anyone's correspondent. Skilled (human Versatile) for the reading she did on her own.
  • Constitution at 14 (the ASI) because Volatile damage is hers to take. Intelligence 18 drives the DC (15) and the pistol.
  • The Cimento (closed 1667) and the Royal Society are real; she and her letters are this game's. The vignette in Euphonia.md's Natural Philosopher chapter (the granary in Pisa) can be her next week.

1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.

Calculated Shot: once per turn, a crossbow or firearm hit on a creature you're studying deals +2d6 of the weapon's damage type. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Hand Crossbows (her pocket pistol counts as one): Vex, so a hit carries Advantage into her next turn.

The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one unless the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest.
