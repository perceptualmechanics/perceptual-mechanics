Background

A younger prince of a minor Hada Rajput house, serving in the Mughal army of the Deccan. He was raised on the Rajput code and the Mughal salary and keeps both without comment. He fences like water and has never lost his temper in public. He reads Persian badly and admits it. Rakhmabai Deshmukh has beaten him at chess twice across a truce table, and he would very much like a third game. What he wants most is to see his name on a deed to land of his own rather than his elder brother's.

How to play

  • He is gracious to everyone, including the people he is about to defeat. Play the apology.
  • In a fight he challenges the most dangerous person in the room, politely, and holds it there. Economy and Example make one perfect cut the party's signal.
  • Out of a fight: The Code and The Hunt, and he is welcome in every camp of the Mughal army.
  • As a rival, a love interest or a gracious enemy, he plays fair. The hook is the land: a party that can help him earn a deed of his own has a sword at its side.

Why this build

  • Why 10th: twenty-seven and already a legend on the practice ground. Example (10th) is the cut that makes his companions brave.
  • College of Arms, as the roster has him: a Rajput gave up books for the sword long ago, and says so in bad Persian.
  • Noble for a prince, Alert for the swing he saw coming.
