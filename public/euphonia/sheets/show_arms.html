Background

Forty-four, Georgian-born, taken into the shah's household as a boy and raised among the ghulams, the slave-soldiers who are the throne's own sword and its officers. He fences, rides, and shoots like a legend, and can't write a couplet to save his life, which the court never lets him forget. He gave the hours to the circle, not the library. When the astrologers ruled the shah's first coronation hour unlucky and a substitute sat the throne to take the bad stars, he was the one who walked the substitute off it before the second crowning. If anyone means to put those stars back on the shah, he stands in the way. He would very much like to hear one ghazal about himself that isn't a joke.

How to play

  • Challenge the biggest thing in the room, then stand in front of whoever it was going for: the Challenge punishes it for swinging at anyone but him, and its circle punishes anyone who steps in.
  • Every turn asks the duelist's question: two cuts, or Economy's one cut, with Advantage, and a free Riposte waiting. Against a foe that swings at his friends, the one cut and the wait usually win.
  • Out of a fight he's the man the quarrels come to (Affairs of Honor), and his old pupils are at every court the shah sends envoys to (A Master's Salle).
  • Don't hand him a letter. He'll take it with great courtesy and give it to the nearest boy.

Why this build

  • Why 11th: the roster's level. A legend at forty-four: Example (10th) is a young officer following him into a breach, and A Master's Salle needs 11th. One Cut (14th) is still ahead of him.
  • College of Arms: he has left Letters to others, and the court has noticed. Goliath from the roster: his grandmother's line from the giants of the Caucasus.
  • Noble for Charisma and Skilled (raised in the shah's household); Strength +1 because a goliath who fences with Charisma still gets asked to lift the gate.
