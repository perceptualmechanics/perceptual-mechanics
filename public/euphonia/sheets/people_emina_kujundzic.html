PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornMostar, in HerzegovinaThe BridgeLevel 7

The Old Bridge at Mostar, a single stone arch over the Neretva, was finished in 1566 for Süleyman the Magnificent, and the town takes its name from its keepers, the mostari, who guarded the bridge and took its tolls. In 1668 Herzegovina is an Ottoman province on the edge of Venetian Dalmatia; Evliya Çelebi, who crossed it in 1664, compared the arch to a rainbow.

On the old bridge at Mostar, a woman in her grandfather's mail plants herself in the middle of the span. The brigand's maul comes down on her friend and lands on her instead. She says his name aloud, the way a clerk enters a name in a register, and when he turns on her he finds every step toward her heavier than the last.

Gear

A spear (+4, 1d6 + 1), a round shield painted with the bridge, her grandfather's mail shirt and coif, the toll book and a reed pen, a lantern for the night watch, a purse of the coins travelers pay in (akçe, Venetian soldi, Ragusan grossi), a small hammer for the bridge's iron clamps. Languages: Bosnian (in place of Common), Ottoman Turkish, Italian, the Ragusan merchants' Latin. Tools: Smith's Tools (Border-Born), Cartographer's Tools (Revelator).

The toll book. Every traveler over the Old Bridge for forty years, in her father's hand and then hers: name, home, what they carried and what they paid. The page for one night in 1656 has a name she can't read; it changes when she looks away.

The shadowless coin. A silver coin with no ruler's face, warm in any weather. Held up to the eye it shows the long bridge. She keeps it sewn into the lining of the mail.

Background

A mostar's daughter of thirty-one, from the family that has kept the Old Bridge since the stone arch went up, who keeps the toll book now that her father's eyes have gone. One night when she was nineteen a traveler crossed at midnight who cast no shadow on the stones. She asked him the toll anyway. He paid it with a coin that showed her the other bridge, the long one over the river of the dead, and she has been able to see it behind the stone one ever since. She enters every traveler's name in the book, and she has never yet been told a false one twice. Her grandfather's mail fits her better than it fitted her brothers, which nobody in the family mentions. She wants her younger sister to marry the boy from the Tabačica quarter, and she wants to know who the shadowless traveler was, and what he was paying for.

How to play

  • Your action is a Planar Strike (or a spell); it deals your open vista's plane's damage. Your Bonus Action opens a vista (a Favor from your pool; two for an Influence that heals; never the same plane two turns running) beside a creature within 60 ft, or commands your spirit. The spirit doesn't fight: it acts on your turn, moving and Helping, and commanded it also uses its plane's Aspect.
  • She stands in the narrow place. Weigh the brigand, Bear the Verdict for the friend he hits, and let the scale make him pay for every blow.
  • The Paperwork: she reads any seal, stamp or form, and after an hour in a customs house she knows whom to see and what each wants. On the frontier between Venice and the sultan, that is a great deal.
  • The toll book and the Assessor: she knows a false name when she hears it, and she writes the true one down.
  • The hook is the shadowless traveler. The name in the book for that night in 1656 changes when she looks away, and something on the long bridge is waiting for the toll to be paid back.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: twelve years since the traveler crossed, the Celestial Bureaucracy, The Paperwork and Lockstep.
  • The Bridge: the Bridge's opening vignette, the woman in her grandfather's mail on the bridge at Mostar, and the first row of its calling table (The Classes, Revelator, the Bridge).
  • Border-Born, for a family that has kept a crossing on a frontier for a century, and the Hidden's border besides. Chain mail and a shield through Bear the Verdict.
