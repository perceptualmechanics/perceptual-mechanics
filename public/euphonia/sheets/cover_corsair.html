Background

A captain of the taifa, the corsair captains' guild, which in Algiers is a constitutional body and keeps better books than most banks. He takes ships; he also ransoms what he takes, and ransoms Muslim captives back out of Malta and Livorno, and he works both sides of the trade because both sides pay. Call him a factor and he'll tell you he's a sea captain; he keeps two sets of books all the same, the guild's and his own. Algiers holds tens of thousands of captives in 1668, and this is a hard part of the real record: a table chooses how much of it to use. Yusuf Rais was born Tom Penhale of Falmouth, an English sailor who "turned Turk": a renegade who took the turban and a new name, and rose to a captain of the taifa.

How to play

  • Most of a boarding is mercenaries. Observe them first and ask who pays them; the ones in it for coin are the ones you can buy off the deck. Anyone else can be offered too, but they came for something other than money.
  • Underwrite one of the crew after a Long Rest: the first time they drop, they get back up with 3d8.
  • Between voyages, the guild's books (Follow the Money, and The House's Books): who bought the captives you're looking for, who paid the bribe, and through whom. Nothing paid by letter of credit crosses Algiers unseen by the taifa.
  • Debts: whoever takes your coin, or your ransom, owes you one reasonable thing, one debt at a time. Call it in.

Why this build

  • Why 10th: a captain of the guild, established: Follow the Money and The House's Books (10th) make him the man who knows where every ransom went. The Market (14th) would make him the guild.
  • Factor, as the Ottoman chapter has it: a Counsellor Factor in all but name. His house is the taifa, a guild, which Letters of Credit allows.
  • Merchant for Intelligence and Lucky. The Talismanic Shirt from Euphonia's Wonders and Curiosities: Ottoman captains wore them into battle.
