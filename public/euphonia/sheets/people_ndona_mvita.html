Soyo, the Kongo province at the mouth of the Congo, is ruled by its own count and trades with the Dutch against the Portuguese at Luanda. The Kongo church has been Catholic for nearly two centuries, with Kongolese catechists, the mestres, teaching in the villages, but the bishops rarely ordain Kongolese priests.

Sundown under the great tree in the plaza at Mbanza Soyo. A woman with a gourd pours palm wine for a Capuchin, a nganga, a Dutch factor and two of the count's shield-bearers, all on one bench. The factor says something about the count. The shield-bearers stiffen. She tops up the factor's cup, says one word in Kikongo, and the bench laughs, the shield-bearers loudest. Nobody remembers afterward what the word was.

Background

A palm-wine seller of Mbanza Soyo whose bench under the great tree is the one place in the province where the Capuchin, the nganga, the Dutch factor and the count's shield-bearers drink together. She pours, listens and repeats nothing; nobody has started a fight at her bench twice. Her son is a mestre who teaches the faith in the villages, and she wants him ordained a priest, which the bishop in Luanda won't do for a Kongolese.

How to play

  • She is an ally and a peacemaker: the person in Soyo who knows who is quarreling with whom.
  • Her bench is neutral ground. A party that needs to meet an enemy without a fight should ask for it.
  • She is no fighter, but nobody has started a fight at her bench twice.
  • The hook is her son. A word from the right person in Luanda, or Rome, would change a great deal.

Why this build

  • Why 5th: twenty years of the bench, and a voice that stops fights.
  • College of Verse: her art is the word at the right moment, and the bench is her stage.
  • Merchant and Lucky for a palm-wine seller; Skilled for everything a bench hears.
