The langar is the Sikh free kitchen: everyone eats sitting in one line on the floor, whatever their rank, caste or faith, and everyone helps cook and clean. In 1668 the ninth Guru, Tegh Bahadur, travels in Bengal and Assam, where Raja Ram Singh's Mughal army is campaigning, and sangats, the congregations, keep langars along his road.

Dusk in the courtyard of the sangat in Dhaka, an iron griddle the size of a cartwheel over the fire. A broad woman in a white headscarf slaps out flatbread with her bare hands, faster than anyone can eat it, while a Mughal trooper, two Bengali boatmen, a Portuguese deserter and an Armenian merchant sit in one line on the mat. She hands the trooper a bowl and points him, without a word, at the pots. He goes and washes them.

Background

The cook of the langar at the Sikh sangat in Dhaka, who has fed Mughal soldiers, Bengali boatmen, Portuguese deserters and an Armenian merchant at one sitting, in one line, and set whoever was nearest to wash the pots. Her husband died guarding the Guru's party on the road from Patna, and she took his sword and his oath with his kirpan, and found the oath took her too: the flour is lighter in her hands, and no one has ever drawn a blade in her courtyard twice. She wants news of the Guru's party on the road to Assam, where her nephew rides as a cook, and whoever brings it eats free for a month.

How to play

  • She is an ally, a host and the safest table in Bengal: everyone eats in one line, and no one draws a blade in her courtyard twice.
  • Feed and be fed. A party that helps wash the pots hears every rumor in Dhaka.
  • In a fight she is a wall with a sword: Protection, Lay on Hands and an oath that won't bend.
  • The hook is the Guru's party on the Assam road. Bring her news of her nephew.

Why this build

  • Why 5th: an oath taken over a husband's body, and kept at a griddle: Extra Attack and Lay on Hands, a quiet strength.
  • Oath of Devotion: service, honesty and a door that is always open.
  • Acolyte for the sangat; Tough for the griddle; Protection, because she stands between her guests and the trouble.
