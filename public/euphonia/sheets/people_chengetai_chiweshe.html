Background

A smith's daughter from a village on the Pungwe who came to Sofala when the village moved for the gold trade, and found the town had no one to keep its edge. So she keeps it. She walks the survey pegs every dusk in the dry months, drives an iron nail where the paving stops and pours a line of salt beside it, and knows which of the pools out on the flats belong to the njuzu and which are only water. The surveyors find her exhausting and the builders find her expensive. The families on the new streets bring her beer. She has buried one surveyor who wouldn't move his pegs, and brought back two children who answered a voice from the reeds. She wants the town council to put the pools on the plan before anyone else drowns, and she wants an apprentice who will listen.

How to play

  • She keeps the town's edge. In her ward the party has Advantage on Initiative and the Hidden have a line of salt and iron to cross first.
  • Read the Tide tells her what is abroad on the flats tonight; Prepare the Ground makes a street corner into a fortress.
  • Mark it, shoot it with iron, and don't follow it into the reeds. She knows which pools to stay out of.
  • Inside Out is for the child who hears a voice from the water and starts walking: a second chance not to answer.
  • The hook is the town plan. Two of next year's streets run through pools that are owed, and someone on the council is being paid to keep them there.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: years on the pegs, and Inside Out: the one who answers the voice from the reeds gets a second chance.
  • Hidden Killer: the Hidden Killer chapter's opening vignette, the Killer on the survey pegs at Sofala (Life on the Border, Hidden Killer Ranger). Fungai of Dambarare, upriver, does the same work.
  • Border-Born, for a smith's daughter who learned iron and the Hidden at the same anvil; Archery, because a Killer who can shoot doesn't have to wade.
