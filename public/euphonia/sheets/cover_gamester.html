Background

A middle-aged French halfling, going gray, who has played at the court's high tables for twenty years: Saint-Germain most weeks, the Tuileries in winter, and Versailles when the king brings the court out for a fête. He has his own chair at his favored table; that is how much of a fixture he is. He knows what Pascal and Fermat wrote to each other about dice, and the court doesn't. Generals, ministers, and bankers want to know the odds, and none of them wants to be seen asking a card player, so they lose to him first and ask after.

How to play

  • Call the attack he likes the odds on: the Fighter's swing at the creature he's Observed, before anyone rolls. A hit gets one more die; a miss costs him only the round, and the first call each night can't lose.
  • Before the job, Honest Assessment: a Deduction on the plan, to ask the odds (5 Deductions a day). Proven right to a patron, he has Advantage with them until he's wrong.
  • Your chair is the best informant at court: the bank knows who is losing, and anyone powerful who plays can find you for a quiet word. Most of the setting's politics passes his table eventually.
  • Everyone's a Mark: one evening at cards and he has a man's bluff, his limit, and his tell for a tenday.

Why this build

  • Why 12th: a fixture, not a rising man. Your Own Chair (10th) is the chair on the cover, and Cold Read (11th) is twenty years of reading the table. All In (14th) is the bet he hasn't made yet.
  • Merchant for Intelligence and Lucky; Lucky and the halfling's Luck are a gambler's whole religion. Alert at 12th, because he likes to see the deal first.
  • Sword cane and pocket pistol from Euphonia's period weapons: both concealable, both what a careful man carries at court.
