The Onondaga are the Firekeepers of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, where the Grand Council meets. A Clan Mother chooses her clan's chief, counsels him and can remove him. The Jogah are the little people of Haudenosaunee story, who must be remembered and thanked; winter is the season when stories may be told. In Euphonia the Jogah are among the Hidden, and the Clan Mother keeps the old courtesies with them.

A longhouse at Onondaga in midwinter, snow on the roof, the fires down the middle. An older woman shells beans with her daughters while a council chief stands before her and explains himself. She doesn't look up. When he is finished she says three sentences, and he sits down. Outside, faint and steady, a drum is beating in the woods where no one is drumming.

Background

An Onondaga Clan Mother, among the Firekeepers of the Haudenosaunee, who chooses her clan's chief for the Grand Council and can remove him. She keeps the old courtesies with the Jogah, the little people, as her grandmothers did, and her prayers are answered by the Hidden in their own way. Somebody has neglected the Jogah, and the drumming has started in the woods with no drummer; she wants it put right so the winter's stories can be told. And she wants her grandson home from the western war.

How to play

  • She is the patron of the drumming (see Hooks), who pays in the Confederacy's remembering.
  • She makes and unmakes chiefs. A party that has her good word is heard at the Grand Council.
  • She bargains with the Hidden the old way: courtesy, offerings, and never a broken promise.
  • The hook is the drum in the woods. Find who neglected the Jogah, and put it right before the stories are due.

Why this build

  • Why 9th: a Clan Mother of the Firekeepers, with Commune for the oldest courtesies.
  • Hidden Domain: the go-between who keeps faith with the little people.
  • Noble for a Clan Mother's standing; Skilled and Healer for a lifetime of keeping a clan.
