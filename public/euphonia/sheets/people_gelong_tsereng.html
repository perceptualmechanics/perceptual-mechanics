PeopleThe Steppe and the NorthErdene Zuu and the herding roundLife DomainLevel 8

In 17th-century Mongolia the Buddhist monasteries campaigned against the shamans and burned the ongon, the felt and wooden figures of spirits kept in the ger. Gelong is a monk's rank. His healing is the Life Domain's; his conscience is his own.

The courtyard of Erdene Zuu, a brazier burning. A monk of forty-five sets a felt ongon on the coals, with real regret, and watches it burn. Then he turns to the woman who made it, takes her hand, which she burned trying to snatch it back, and bandages it, gently, saying a prayer for her.

Background

A Khalkha Buddhist monk of Erdene Zuu who heals the sick on the herding round and burns the shamans' ongon, because he honestly believes they hold spirits that harm children. He enforces the law gently and without exception, and keeps one ongon he couldn't bring himself to burn in a box under his bed. He wants to be thanked by a shaman, once, for a healing; he knows it's unreasonable.

How to play

  • He is a foil or an ally: kind, sincere, and burning the things the shamans hold sacred.
  • He heals everyone, shaman or not. That is the whole of his contradiction.
  • The ongon under his bed is the hook. Whoever finds it learns more about him than the abbot knows.
  • Let a shaman thank him, once. See what he does.

Why this build

  • Why 8th: twenty years on the herding round.
  • Life Domain, as the roster has him: a healer first.
  • Acolyte and Healer for a monastery's medicine.
