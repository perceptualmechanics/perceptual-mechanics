Wäldä Mika'el

Human · Scribe · Wizard 7 (Abjurer) · level 7
On the coverGondar, 1668The year of Yohannes I's council

The däbtära are the region's learned magicians: unordained church scholars, cantors and poets, trained for decades in Ge'ez, chant and qene, the poetry of double meaning. They write God's secret names (asmat) on the skin of an animal killed in the patient's place, the client's name in red, and cut the scroll to the client's height. In Euphonia the asmat are this region's science of letters, Hermeticism's cousin. In 1668 Yohannes I's council is ruling on who belongs inside Gondar's walls.

In a Gondar courtyard a child is screaming at something no one else can see. The däbtära unrolls the scroll he finished at dawn, the length of the child, the names in red, and the thing on the child's chest finds itself standing inside the lines of an eight-pointed star it can't get out of. He begins to sing.

A healing scroll, cut to the client's height. Written on the skin of an animal killed in the client's place, God's secret names in Ge'ez and the client's name in red, with the Net of Solomon and St. Susenyos riding down Werzelya. In play it works as a Spell Scroll of Protection from Evil and Good (Common), scribed by him, that only the person named on it can use. He writes one for a client in a day of work; the one on this sheet is for a client, and it is the height of a man.

The Net of Solomon. The eight-pointed star the scrolls trap demons in. When he casts Magic Circle, he draws it as the Net. In Euphonia the demons are real, diabolical, and the saints fight them; the däbtära draw the lines the saints ride inside.

Background

Thirty years in the church schools: reading, then chant, then qene, then the commentaries and the reckoning of the feasts. He was never ordained and never meant to be; he sings at the services, argues unction with the monks from Gojjam over coffee, and writes scrolls for anyone who pays, Christian, Muslim or Beta Israel, which this year's council has made a more complicated thing to do. The church tolerates his work because the texts are Christian. He has heard that an old scholar at Enfraz is testing every religion by reason, and he would like very much to argue with him.

How to play

  • Your work is protection: the ward on you, the circle around the thing, the scroll on the client. Every Abjuration you cast tops up the ward, so cast them.
  • Projected Ward makes you the party's shield: when the fighter takes the hit, your ward takes it instead.
  • Remove Curse, Protection from Evil and Good and Banishment are what clients pay for. Out of a fight, you are the one people come to when a child sickens.
  • You are a cantor and a poet. Musician gives your friends Heroic Inspiration after every rest; qene, the wax and the gold, is how you say two things at once at court.
  • Gondar is sorting who belongs inside the wall this year. Your clients are on both sides of the line.

Why this build

  • Wizard: the book's call that the asmat are this region's science of letters, Hermeticism's cousin, makes the däbtära a learned caster. Abjurer: the scrolls are protection, head to toe, and the Net traps demons; the Geomancer is the Hermetic copy of the fae's space and time and doesn't fit.
  • Level 7: decades of schooling, and a working scholar-healer with a level 4 spell (Banishment), not a court magus.
  • Scribe (Int +2, Wis +1; Skilled) for the man who writes; Musician (human Versatile) for the cantor.
  • Intelligence 18: DC 15, and the ward at 18 HP.
