John Winthrop the Younger, Governor of Connecticut since 1659, was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1663, the first from the American colonies; he kept a great chemical library, practiced physic for half the colony by letter and wrote to Henry Oldenburg in London. Wethersfield, on the Connecticut River below Hartford, stands on Wangunk land the Wangunk call Pyquag. In 1668 the town is talking about the widow Katherine Harrison, whom neighbors have accused of witchcraft.

A March morning on the Broad Street green in Wethersfield, the frost still on the grass. The lean-to behind the Crane house has no roof. A selectman stands at the gate with his hat in his hands, and in the yard a lean man with no eyebrows is patting out a smoldering cuff with his own hat. "That," the man says, "was the expected result." Behind him, his sister sets down an empty bucket of sand and goes to fetch another.

The commonplace book. Every experiment since 1651, the method, the weather and the result. The results column reads "expected" more often than it should. The last page is a draft letter to London, begun eleven times.

Thankful's buckets. Not his. Sand, one inside every door of the house, refilled every Saturday. She has never once had to tell him where they are.

Background

A Wethersfield maltster's son who learned his chemistry as a young man tending the furnaces at John Winthrop's house in New London, and kept at it after the governor moved up the river to Hartford. The governor lends him books, and the Royal Society's Transactions when a ship brings them; Eliphalet reads them twice and then tries the experiment. He has taken the roof off his elaboratory twice, once with niter and once with the fulminating gold the books warn about in the same paragraph as they describe it. The town has fined him twice, the minister has preached on him once, and his sister Thankful keeps a bucket of sand inside every door. He wants to send London one paper under his own name that ends in a result and not a fire, and he would like the next sermon to say niter and not the Devil.

How to play

  • Two experiments a day, the energy chosen as he mixes it. Thunder clears a doorway; Lightning's Line is for men in iron; Cold slows a pursuit; Fire is the one the town is worried about.
  • Overproof when it has to work, and Volatile doubles its odds. He knows the odds. He has written them down.
  • Demonstration: an hour, an audience and a bell going silent in an emptied jar, and for a day everyone who watched is easier to persuade. He uses it on selectmen.
  • Touchstone: he can tell what a metal, a salt or a powder is, and whether it is what its seller says. Half of Hartford brings him their coin and their medicine to test.
  • The hook is the letter to London: help him get one experiment to come out the same twice, and witness it, and he will put your names in the paper.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: twenty years at the furnace, Overproof and Touchstone. Insulation (10th), Resistance to his own experiments, is a long way off, which is what the eyebrows are about.
  • Experimentalist: the Pitch's "New England experimenter trying not to blow up his home (again)" (The Setting, Welcome to Euphonia, The Pitch).
  • Artisan and Crafter for a maltster's son who builds his own apparatus; Skilled for the governor's books. A pistol for Ballistics, because a New England householder keeps one.
  • John Winthrop the Younger is real, and acts here only as he did: governor, Fellow, correspondent, lender of books. Eliphalet and his letters are this game's.

Natural Philosopher (Experimentalist)
1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.
1Ballistics. Crossbows and firearms (Pistol, Musket, the period firearms; the stonebow is a crossbow) can use Int for attack and damage. Calculated Shot: once per turn, a crossbow or firearm hit on a creature you're studying deals +2d6 of the weapon's damage type. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Pistols: Vex, so a hit carries Advantage into his next attack against the same creature.
1Aim. When you study a creature on your turn, your next attack roll against it this turn has Advantage if it's a crossbow or firearm attack using your Intelligence. 2First Causes. A minute examining something physical, then ask the DM why it happened (or is happening): a sentence or two on the likeliest cause that can be seen, measured, or tested. Too little to go on, the DM says so and the use is kept. Never about minds or feelings. 4 uses per Long Rest.
2Instruments. Expertise with two of your tools, or with one tool and Investigation.
3Mechanism. Finding the feature: study the place (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight) and the DM names one feature you could set off (part of the place: nothing you or your allies placed, lit, or made), the laws it allows, and its reach; you're now studying it. Every place has at least one (at worst the ground itself: loose stones, a cut bank, a rotten floor; Mechanics); a rich place has two or three (study again for the next). Reach: 30 ft from the feature unless the DM names more (a dam, a powder store, a millrace can reach farther). Setting it off: Utilize action, one precise act from where you stand (or at a distance: a shot, a thrown stone, a tug on a line already rigged); choose one of the named laws. Place a 20-ft Cube, origin within 60 ft; chosen creatures that are in the Cube and within the feature's reach save, DC 15. Fail: 4d8 and the law's effect. Success: half damage only. The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one unless the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest. Mechanics (a load, slope, hanging weight): Dex, Bludgeoning; Restrained (pinned) until the end of its next turn; rubble (Difficult Terrain). Heat (fire, furnace, powder, sealed vessel): Dex, Fire; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn; flammables catch fire. Pneumatics (gas, smoke, foul air): Con, Poison; Poisoned until the end of its next turn; Heavily Obscured until the end of yours. Hydrostatics (water held back): Str, Bludgeoning; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Prone; Difficult Terrain for 1 minute. Optics (strong light and a lens, mirror, or glass of the place): Con, Radiant; Blinded until the end of its next turn.

3Demonstration. An hour to set up and an audience: a harmless, plainly unmagical demonstration (sparks from a rubbed globe, a bell falling silent in an emptied jar, water frozen on a summer afternoon). 5Out of the Line. Reaction when a creature you can see hits you and you aren't in Medium or Heavy armor: halve that damage (round down).

6Touchstone. Alchemist's Supplies and 10 minutes: what a metal, alloy, salt, or acid is and how pure (a clipped or debased coin, adulterated gold and with what, a powder that isn't what its seller says). You tell platina from silver, which almost no one outside Mutapa can yet do reliably, and you know what that's worth to the people who'd pay.
