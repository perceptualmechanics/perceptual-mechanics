The Āl is a thin old woman with a clay nose who wants a new mother's liver: one of the low pari that took a demon's name. In Tabriz the forty days after a birth are kept against her with iron, onions, garlic and prayer, and every quarter of the city has its keeper of the forty days. Nane is a Hidden Killer by another name.

A courtyard in Tabriz, past midnight, the new mother asleep behind the door. An old woman on a stool across the doorway cracks pistachios, an iron sickle on her knees. Something thin, with a nose of clay, looks round the courtyard gate. Nane doesn't get up. She says its name, the real one, quite conversationally, and it goes. Then she goes back to the pistachios, and a few minutes later, to sleep.

Iron and Salt (Adventuring Gear, cheap and common). Iron in the cradle and the doorway: a sickle, a needle, a horseshoe, and onions and garlic hung over the lintel for the forty days. Iron fixed at a door, window or chimney, with a line of salt across it, wards that opening until the next dawn. Ten minutes wards every opening of a room or small building (a great house: 10 minutes a floor or wing), and while every opening is warded a Hidden creature can't come in through any of them unless a creature inside that isn't Hidden asks it in aloud (freely, for pay, by a lie or Charmed: it counts; it admits the one asked and its company, until dawn or until taken back; a lodger's can last the stay). No save crosses a ward. An unwarned householder who hears a voice at the door makes a Wisdom save against the asker's spell save DC (DC 13 if the asker has none; Advantage while the Hidden are at the ebb) and asks it in on a failure; one told plainly what is asking succeeds unless Charmed. Moving the iron or breaking the salt ends the ward on that opening (a Utilize action for anyone who isn't Hidden; no Hidden creature can do it by hand, by a thrown object or by magic), and a new hole has no ward until someone gives it one. Iron and salt bar only the openings they line: teleportation or a Door that makes its own way in isn't barred (a Hidden Killer's ward stops that). In Tabriz the salt goes in a ring around the mother's bed as well, and Nane renews it every evening at the call to prayer.

The iron needle. A long iron needle in her headscarf. She put it through the Āl's shadow once, thirty years ago. She has never needed to again, because the Āl remembers.

Background

A Tabriz midwife who keeps the forty days after a birth against the Āl, and has never lost a mother to her. She has seen the Āl eleven times in forty years and put an iron needle through her shadow once. She sits across a new mother's doorway through the night, an iron sickle on her knees and onions and garlic over the lintel, and when something looks round the gate, she says its name without getting up. She says the trick isn't courage, it's staying awake, and then falls asleep in the middle of saying so. At sixty-three she wants an apprentice whose knees are younger than hers and who isn't afraid of the dark.

How to play

  • Most nights nothing comes, and her job is to stay awake. When something does come, she names it. The book's Āl has a name; let the party hear her say it.
  • Her fights are short and close: the ward, the mark, two iron needles and the Āl runs. She is sixty-three and doesn't chase.
  • Out of a fight she is a midwife first: Healer, Medicine with Expertise, Lesser Restoration and Protection from Poison. A party that needs a doctor in Tabriz who asks no questions has found one.
  • The hook is the apprentice. A player character who isn't afraid of the dark may find themselves on a stool across a doorway for forty nights.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: forty years at doorways, and Inside Out at 7th: the mother who has been Charmed into opening the door gets a second chance.
  • Hermit for Healer and Medicine: a midwife's life. Thrown Weapon Fighting for the iron needles, which she has thrown at a shadow exactly once.
  • A human, as the roster has her: the Killer's craft is learned, and in Tabriz it is passed from one midwife to the next.
