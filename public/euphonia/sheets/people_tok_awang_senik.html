Tok Awang Senik

Human · Guide · Cleric 6 (Hidden) · level 6
PeopleEast and Southeast AsiaThe Kedah plain, below Gunung JeraiHidden DomainLevel 6

In Kedah the old people count the orang bunian, the hidden people of the forest, among the Hidden and not among the jinn (Life on the Border, Hidden Domain Cleric); Gunung Jerai, the great mountain over the Kedah plain, is their country in the stories. The sultanate sends the gold and silver flowers to Ayutthaya as its tribute, trades pepper and tin with whoever comes, and keeps its bomoh.

The edge of the forest below Gunung Jerai at dusk, the rice behind him gone gray. An old man in a faded baju steps in under the trees with a betel quid wrapped in a leaf, talking softly to the dark as if to an old neighbor, about a boy who answered a whistle he shouldn't have. Somewhere ahead there are lamps where no village should be, and music, and a child laughing.

Gear

A betel box of worked brass, a supply of betel leaf and areca nut, a keris he never draws in a parley, a pair of betel scissors for under a child's pillow, a lamp, a faded baju and sarong, a carved wooden comb from the bunian village, which he has never been able to give back. Languages: Malay (in place of Common), Thai (the court's, a little), Arabic (the prayers), Sylvan (the Go-Between: the bunian's speech as Kedah hears it). Tools: Cartographer's Tools (Guide), which he has never used, because the forest moves.

The comb. Carved wood, from the bunian village, given to him at fourteen by a girl there who is still sixteen. He has tried to return it four times. Each time the house where he left it was gone in the morning, and the comb was in his pocket.

The betel scissors. Iron, the Malay household's ward: a pair under a child's pillow keeps the bunian's whistle out of her dreams. He hands out new pairs every Ramadan.

Background

A bomoh of a village on the Kedah plain, sixty-three, thin and unhurried, who was taken by the orang bunian at fourteen and sent back with the way out. Their villages are like Kedah's, only quieter, kinder and never quite where you left them, and children who hear the whistle go to look. When that happens, the family comes to Tok Awang. He knows the courtesies: what to offer, what not to eat, which name not to say. He usually comes back with the child before morning. The bunian have offered to keep him every time; it is a kind offer, and he says so, and says not yet. He wants someone to take his place, someone patient enough, before the forest stops taking no for an answer.

How to play

  • He talks first, always, and he is very good at it: Bargains gives him Advantage on reading the bunian and on striking a bargain, with Wisdom.
  • Bring Back is what he goes into the forest for: a Charmed child comes back to herself.
  • The Tide tells him when the bunian are near and abroad, and he goes in when they are faint and far off if he can.
  • Play him as kind, slow and amused, the way a man is when he has said no to paradise every year for fifty years.
  • The hook is a successor. A party member with patience and good manners might be offered the betel box.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: fifty years of courtesies, and Rowan and Red Thread.
  • Hidden Domain: the Hidden Domain chapter's opening vignette, the Kedah bomoh going into the forest at dusk after a boy who answered a whistle (Life on the Border, Hidden Domain Cleric).
  • Guide for a man who goes into the forest and comes back; Magic Initiate (Druid) for the birds.
