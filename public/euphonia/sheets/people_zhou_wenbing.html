The Wutong, five sinister gods of Jiangnan, are handsome gentlemen who give husbands sudden wealth in exchange for their wives, then take it back at any slight (The Setting, East and Southeast Asia, The Hidden Here). In Euphonia they are of the Hidden; a Jiangnan husband the Wutong made rich is a Warlock, and the price is his wife (The Setting, Characters, Classes in 1668). Suzhou is Jiangnan's richest city, of silk, canals and gardens, ten years into Qing rule.

A new house on a Suzhou canal, the paint still bright, the garden still too young. A man in a silk robe finer than he can carry off stands in the courtyard at dusk with a cup of wine he hasn't drunk, looking at the five empty chairs he has had set at the table every night for a year. Upstairs, his wife has barred her door. When the lanterns are lit, he pours five more cups, and his hand shakes.

The purse. Silk, embroidered with five horses. Whatever he takes out of it, there is more in it the next morning. He has started giving it away to beggars in the hope the gentlemen will count it as a slight. They haven't, yet.

The five cups. Set out every night at the table for the gentlemen, filled and left. In the morning they are empty. One night one of them was only half drunk, and the next day a ship of his went down off Ningbo.

Background

A Suzhou silk broker of thirty-six who was poor two years ago, and drunk one night, and made a bargain with five handsome gentlemen at a wayside shrine that he didn't understand until the money began to arrive. It has not stopped arriving: a ship comes in, a debtor pays, a bale he bought for nothing sells in Nanjing for a fortune. He has a new house on the canal, a garden and a rank he bought. The gentlemen visit. They are courteous to him, and they look at his wife, Lin Shi, the way a buyer looks at goods paid for. He loves her, which he didn't know he did until he understood what he'd sold. She has barred her door and is looking for someone to break the bargain before the gentlemen come for her. He wants the same thing, and he is afraid to say so out loud, because the gentlemen take back what they gave at any slight, and they will hear.

How to play

  • He is a patron who pays very well and can't say why. Everything he touches turns to money, and he hates it.
  • Manners: he knows the Hidden's courtesies, whether a bargain they offer is binding, and he bargains with them at Advantage. It hasn't helped.
  • Step into the Hidden and Come With Me: if the gentlemen come for Lin Shi, he means to take her and run. He doesn't know where to.
  • Play the fear: he can't say what he wants aloud, because the gentlemen take back what they gave at any slight, and they will hear.
  • The hook is the bargain (The Setting, East and Southeast Asia, Hooks: A Wutong gift). Lin Shi will hire the party; he will pay them twice, secretly, and pretend he never did.

Why this build

  • Why 5th: two years of the gentlemen's money, and level 3 slots for Hypnotic Pattern and Counterspell.
  • Dreamlord: Classes in 1668, Warlock: "In Jiangnan the Wutong make a husband rich, and the price is his wife" (The Setting, Characters). The Wutong are of the Hidden in Euphonia, so his patron is the Realm in their five faces: a judgment call, marked for Scott.
  • Merchant and Skilled for a silk broker; Alert, because a man in his position is always listening for the gentlemen at the door.
