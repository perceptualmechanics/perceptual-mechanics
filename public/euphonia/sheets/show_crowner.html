A Boston coroner who views every sudden death where it lies, and keeps finding that the people said to have been bewitched died of something with a name. New England hangs witches; there are real ones, which is what makes it hard.

A lane off the Boston waterfront, early, frost on the cobbles. The dead man lies where he fell, and the magistrate standing over him is quoting Exodus. The coroner kneels without answering, holds a brass rule to the dead man's eye, then turns his hand over and looks at the nails for a long time. He writes down the magistrate's verse, and the width of the pupil, on the same page, and asks the jury of neighbors which of them sold the man his bread.

Background

Josiah Thorne was elected coroner by the freeholders of his county for life, which is longer than some of them now wish. He views the body before anyone may bury it, summons a jury of neighbors, hears the first finder and whoever else will talk, and returns a verdict. Too many of the verdicts the colony would like are witchcraft, and too many of the bodies he views died of rot, fever, ergot or a poisoner with a grudge. He believes there are witches; he has met one. He has also come to believe New England hangs mostly the wrong ones, and wants one verdict of witchcraft overturned in the General Court with his reasons read aloud. He writes to the Royal Society about poisons, fevers and rot and has not been answered. He went to Onondaga once on a colony's errand and came back with more questions than the errand allowed for.

How to play

  • Your best work happens before and after the fight. Reconstruction (10 minutes, the last day) says how many, what they did, when, and which way they went; The View (10 minutes with a body) says when, how, and whether it was moved. Neither says who: that's yours.
  • In a fight, Find the Flaw makes the studied creature easier for everyone: AC down 1 for as long as you study it, so restudying each turn keeps it down (and lets you switch to a Resistance). Study, then let the party swing first.
  • Standing (6th) is a level away: the bench will have to hear you out. For now you have the office, which lets you view any body before burial and summon a jury.
  • Healer: you kneel by the living before the dead. Battle Medic turns a Healer's Kit into a Hit Point Die and your Proficiency Bonus.
  • Real witches exist in Euphonia, which is why the colony is so sure. Your job is the cases where the evidence says something else, and the nerve to write it down.

Why this build

  • Level 5, as the roster has him: a county coroner, a careful man, not a famous one. Calculated Shot and Mechanism step up here, and Out of the Line keeps him alive in a lane; What It Is and Standing (6th) are next.
  • Scribe (Int +2, Wis +1; Skilled) because the job is writing it down. Healer (human Versatile) because a coroner is also the nearest man with a kit. Observant at 4th for Perception Expertise: nothing on the body escapes him (passive Perception 17).
  • Instruments took Investigation with Alchemist's Supplies at 2nd, so Reconstruction's Expertise went to Medicine: the one Crowner in New England who reads a body like a physician.
  • The pistol rather than a musket: a coroner walks into rooms, not onto battlefields.

Features

Natural Philosopher (Crowner)
1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.
1Ballistics. Crossbows and firearms (Pistol, Musket, the period firearms; the stonebow is a crossbow) can use Int for attack and damage. Calculated Shot: once per turn, a crossbow or firearm hit on a creature you're studying deals +2d6 of the weapon's damage type. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Pistols: Vex, so a hit carries Advantage into his next turn.
1Aim. When you study a creature on your turn, your next attack roll against it this turn has Advantage if it's a crossbow or firearm attack using your Intelligence. Move as you like before or after; restudying the same creature counts, so you can Aim every turn. Breathe out, and let the number settle.
2First Causes. A minute examining something physical, then ask the DM why it happened (or is happening): a sentence or two on the likeliest cause that can be seen, measured, or tested. Too little to go on, the DM says so and the use is kept. Never about minds or feelings. 4 uses per Long Rest.
2Instruments. Expertise with two of your tools, or with one tool and Investigation.
3Mechanism. Finding the feature: study the place (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight) and the DM names one feature you could set off (part of the place: nothing you or your allies placed, lit, or made), the laws it allows, and its reach; you're now studying it. Every place has at least one (at worst the ground itself: loose stones, a cut bank, a rotten floor; Mechanics); a rich place has two or three (study again for the next). Reach: 30 ft from the feature unless the DM names more (a dam, a powder store, a millrace can reach farther). Setting it off: Utilize action, one precise act from where you stand (or at a distance: a shot, a thrown stone, a tug on a line already rigged); choose one of the named laws. Place a 20-ft Cube, origin within 60 ft; chosen creatures that are in the Cube and within the feature's reach save, DC 15. Fail: 4d8 and the law's effect. Success: half damage only. The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one unless the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest. Mechanics (a load, slope, hanging weight): Dex, Bludgeoning; Restrained (pinned) until the end of its next turn; rubble (Difficult Terrain). Heat (fire, furnace, powder, sealed vessel): Dex, Fire; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn; flammables catch fire. Pneumatics (gas, smoke, foul air): Con, Poison; Poisoned until the end of its next turn; Heavily Obscured until the end of yours. Hydrostatics (water held back): Str, Bludgeoning; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Prone; Difficult Terrain for 1 minute. Optics (strong light and a lens, mirror, or glass of the place): Con, Radiant; Blinded until the end of its next turn.
3Reconstruction. Expertise in Investigation (proficiency first if needed; another proficient skill if you already have it). Ten minutes at a place where something happened within the last day: the DM tells you, broadly, how many were there, what they did, about when, and which way they went.
3The View. Ten minutes with a body: how long it has been dead (to the hour within a day, to a few days after), what killed it, whether it was moved after death, whether it was alive when it went into the water or the fire, and what made the wounds (the kind of blade, shot, or blow, and from which side). Nothing about who, beyond what the wounds alone say.
3Find the Flaw. When you study a creature, choose one, lasting as long as you're studying it: its AC drops by 1, or it loses a Resistance you pick (you learn its Resistances). Studying it again lets you choose again; flaws don't stack.
5Out of the Line. Reaction when a creature you can see hits you and you aren't in Medium or Heavy armor: halve that damage (round down).
