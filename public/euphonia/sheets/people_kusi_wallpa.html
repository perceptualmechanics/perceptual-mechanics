In Euphonia the Inca state survives in the highlands and fights Spain along the Apurímac. A khipu is a record of knotted cords: counts of people, storehouses and tribute, kept by trained readers.

A cold rock above the road a Spanish column must take, the Apurímac far below. A man of fifty-two sits with a khipu across his knees, tying knots without looking at the cord, eyes on a smudge of dust on the far ridge. A runner shifts from foot to foot behind him. "Two hundred," he says, "forty horse, six guns, and they have not eaten since yesterday." He ties one more knot. "Empty the storehouse at Limatambo by tonight."

Background

The Sapa Inca's senior staff officer on the Apurímac front, who reads Spanish columns by their dust and empties the storehouses on their road before they arrive. He has never lost a column he could see, and he goes to great lengths to see them all. He hires outsiders for the jobs a khipu can't do: reading a Spanish captain, buying a mule-driver, finding out who in Potosí is selling powder to both sides. He pays in cloth, safe roads and his word, and his word is the only one of the three he has never had to replace. He wants one season in which nobody he has counted into a khipu has to be counted out of it.

How to play

  • He wins before the fight: Staff Officer, Lay of the Land, Logistics and Siegecraft. The Spanish column arrives at an empty storehouse.
  • He is a patron who hires outsiders for what a khipu can't do. His word is good.
  • Deduction, in a minute: "Which pass will they take after the rains?" He is usually right.
  • Play him tired and exact. Every knot is a person.

Why this build

  • Why 13th: the senior staff officer of the front, with Faster Than Thought, Siegecraft and Closed Mind. Terms and Fire Control (14th) are a year away.
  • Marshal, as the roster has him: he has never lost a column he could see.
  • Guard and Alert for a lifetime on the Inca roads.
