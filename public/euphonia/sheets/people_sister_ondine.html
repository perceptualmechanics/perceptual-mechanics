Lanternhall is Europe's school of the healing arts in Basel, where the Physicians train. Its night sisters are not Physicians and cast no spells: they nurse, watch, feed and decide, and the Hall runs on them. She has no magic at all, and the Majordomo needs none.

Three in the morning on the Long Ward, one lamp lit. A small, square woman in a grey habit moves down the beds with a cup of water, and stops at one where a patient has asked for it in a certain way. She sits down beside him and stays. On her way back she finds a Physician asleep on his feet against a pillar, takes his elbow, steers him to an empty bed and puts a blanket over him, and he lets her.

The night book. Every patient on the Long Ward, how they asked for water, and what happened. The Physicians read it in the morning and don't argue with it.

Background

The senior night sister on Lanternhall's Long Ward. She can't cast a spell, and she is the one person in the Hall every Physician obeys. She knows which patients will die by the way they ask for water, and which students will break by the way they say they're fine. She has threatened Ilyanor Sael with a blanket; she wants to see him sleep in a bed, once, with his eyes shut. She is the ward's conscience and its comedy, and she sends exhausted students to bed whether they like it or not.

How to play

  • She is an ally, the ward's conscience and its comedy. Every Physician in the Hall does what she says.
  • She has no magic, and doesn't need it: Observe, Direct and a healer's kit on a ward full of casters.
  • She knows which patients will die, and which students are about to break. Ask her, quietly.
  • The hook is Ilyanor Sael and the blanket. Get him to sleep in a bed, once, and she will be your friend for life.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: thirty years on the Long Ward, and the Hall runs on her.
  • Majordomo: the ward is her household, and every cupboard has her key in it.
  • Hermit and Healer, with no magic at all, as the book has her (she can't cast a spell); Alert for the night watch.
