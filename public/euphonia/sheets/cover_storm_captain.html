The Ya'rubi imams of Oman drove the Portuguese from Muscat in 1650, are building a navy, and raid Portuguese posts down the Swahili coast; Fort Jesus at Mombasa is watching the horizon. The monsoon runs the ocean: dhows sail to East Africa on the northeast wind and home in late spring. Ships' captains want a Storm sorcerer aboard. This one is the captain.

Off Mombasa, with Fort Jesus's guns run out and the Portuguese galleon closing, the wind drops dead and every man on the dhow looks aft at the captain. His hair is standing up. The compass on the binnacle is pointing at him. He says, in a voice they hear from the bow, "Hold on to something," and the sky over the galleon goes white.

Elemental-blood (air)

Elemental-blood (air): Resistance to Lightning; Light on the Air (Speed 35 ft, jumps doubled); The Old Shape (from 3rd: Bonus Action, 1 minute, once per Long Rest, twice from 5th: a Fly Speed equal to his Speed and hover, and the first attack roll against him each round has Disadvantage); Kin of the Sphere; Elementalism on air only. No ability increases. Elemental-bloods are beings of the elements that took a human shape to cross over and forgot they had. In Muscat that is a story about a great-grandmother and a sandstorm; in a Portuguese port it would be a case for the Inquisition. The lightning on the mast woke what he was.

Gear

A walking staff of blackened, lightning-split wood as his Arcane Focus; a khanjar at his belt (as a dagger); a kamal, the knotted cord and wooden card that measure a star's height, and a book of sailing directions in the line of Ibn Majid; a share in his dhow and command of its crew of thirty; a chest of Gulf dates and a bolt of indigo cloth for trade; three cords of wind-knots for sale; a lump of fused sand from the beach where the lightning first found him. Languages: Arabic (in place of Common), Swahili and Persian, and the Portuguese every captain on this coast swears in. Tools: Navigator's Tools (Merchant).

Wind-Knots. Three knots in a cord, tied on each Long Rest: whoever unties one before his next Long Rest gets a fair wind for an hour, whatever the weather. He sells them in every port for a price that rises with the season. Untied after his next Long Rest, a knot does nothing; the buyers learn this once.

Background

Rashid bin Khamis al-Ghafiri is a merchant's son of Muscat, born under the Portuguese guns and twelve when the imam's men took the forts. It came through him at sea, the first time: lightning hit the mast and climbed down it to him, and didn't stop there: it woke the air in his blood, the elemental that took a human shape generations back and forgot. The crew wouldn't touch him for a week. The owner made him captain within a year, because a ship with the storm aboard makes every passage on time. Now he sails the monsoon to Pate, Zanzibar and Kilwa with dates and Gulf horses outbound and ivory, mangrove poles and Mutapan curiosities home, and the imam's officers have started asking whether his dhow might carry guns instead. He sells knotted cords to other captains at every port. The bell-ringers of the Portuguese churches, who ring against storms, don't like him at all.

How to play

  • Your Pressure is a clock. It starts every fight at 1 and rises at the end of every turn, Release or not; it reaches 3 at the end of your second turn, and on your third you Release on the creature that matters: three dice and the Forked Strike. Keep the die where the table can see it.
  • Cast every turn. A cantrip counts: Sorcerous Burst or Shocking Grasp can carry a Release when your slots are for the weather.
  • The air in your blood moves you: Speed 35 ft across a pitching deck. The Old Shape, twice a day, lifts you off it for a minute: you fly, and the first blow each round has Disadvantage.
  • Your voice carries 300 ft and you know tomorrow's weather: you are the best captain on the coast before you cast a spell.
  • Thunderclap and Resistance to Lightning and Thunder make you the safest man on a deck in a storm, including your own. Stand where the boarders come over.
  • Stormcharged is how you board: charged, you cross the gap between hulls in the air and walk the enemy rail without a boarder getting a swing. Take it the turn after Innate Sorcery, or when the decks close.
  • Wind-Knots are your trade as much as your magic. A captain who sells fair winds knows every other captain on the Monsoon Road.

Why this build

  • Storm Origin, Euphonia's: ships' captains want a Storm sorcerer aboard, and Thunderclap and Wind-Knots at 6th are a sea captain's features.
  • Level 7: a captain in his prime with Ice Storm and Conjure Minor Elementals in hand, not yet the imam's admiral.
  • Merchant (Cha +2, Con +1; Lucky): a dhow captain is a trader first. Elemental-blood (air): his element is the storm he calls; the lightning on the mast woke what he was.
  • Out of Muscat: the brief offers the Swahili coast or Muscat; Muscat puts the 1668 war (Oman against the Portuguese, down the Swahili coast) on the cover, and his voyages reach the Swahili towns anyway.
