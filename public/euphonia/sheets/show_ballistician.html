A Flemish Jesuit lay brother in Beijing who can cast a cannon, mend a clock and compute a noon shadow, and is checking every figure he can before December, when the Bureau's noon shadow is to be measured in front of the court and someone's calendar will be found wrong.

The courtyard behind the Jesuit chapel, a quarter-hour before noon. A lay brother in a dusty cassock kneels on the flagstones with a plumb line, a chalk mark and a slate covered in figures, and when the Bureau's clerk starts to speak he holds up one hand without looking round. The shadow of the gnomon crawls toward the chalk. It stops a hair short. He says something in Flemish that a lay brother shouldn't, wipes the slate, and starts again.

Background

Joris Vanhove grew up in Mechelen with the foundry's noise in his ears, carried powder for a battery at twelve, and counted how far each shot fell. The Society took him as a lay brother for his hands, not his Latin, which is clumsy, and sent him east because the mission in Beijing had a calendar to keep and cannon to cast for an emperor, and needed someone who could do both without complaint. He mends clocks for anyone who knocks at the workshop behind the chapel. He likes the Bureau's Chinese astronomers better than the Portuguese fathers and says so in confession. He writes to the Republic of Letters and gets answers within three years. The gnomon trial this winter is the most honest test he will ever see, and he is terrified of it, because his figures are part of it.

How to play

  • Study first, then shoot: Calculated Shot, Aim and Disabling Shot all want the creature you're studying. Cover, range and moving don't matter to you; the study does. Run the gallery for the angle, study, and shoot.
  • Disabling Shot is the Jesuit's shot: not where it hurts most, where it costs most. Take the linstock out of a hand, or pin a man in place for the rest of the party.
  • Give him a cannon and he aims it with his Intelligence (Gunnery); give him ten minutes with a wall and he tells you how long it stands (Master Gunner).
  • First Causes (4 a day): a minute with a burst barrel, a cracked bell or a clock that loses an hour a week, and the DM tells him why.
  • Fireworks: a day and 25 GP of powder and salts make a display the court will remember, and six signal rockets in a code of his own. The Bureau's own pyrotechnists taught him more than he taught them.

Why this build

  • Level 7, as the roster has him: a working foundryman-astronomer, not a famous one. Leverage (7th) is the man who moves a cannon with a lever and a rope; Correspondence (9th) and Through and Through (10th, every studied hit can pass into the man behind) are still ahead, which suits a man whose letters take three years.
  • Artisan (Int +2, Dex +1; Crafter) because he came to the Society from a foundry, not a college. Skilled for the Jesuit schooling he did get: Religion, Medicine, Arcana.
  • Intelligence 18 runs the musket (Ballistics) and the Mechanism DC; Dexterity 16 goes into the buff coat (AC 15) so Out of the Line stays open.
  • The musket and the quadrant are the period instruments of his trade: Tartaglia invented the gunner's quadrant for this work. Real people near him (the Flemish father he serves, the Bureau's astronomers) appear only by name, if at all.

1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.
1Ballistics. Crossbows and firearms (Pistol, Musket, the period firearms; the stonebow is a crossbow) can use Int for attack and damage. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Muskets: Slow, so a hit cuts the target's Speed by 10 ft.

2First Causes. A minute examining something physical, then ask the DM why it happened (or is happening): a sentence or two on the likeliest cause that can be seen, measured, or tested. Too little to go on, the DM says so and the use is kept. Never about minds or feelings. 4 uses per Long Rest.
2Instruments. Expertise with two of your tools, or with one tool and Investigation.
3Mechanism. Finding the feature: study the place (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight) and the DM names one feature you could set off (part of the place: nothing you or your allies placed, lit, or made), the laws it allows, and its reach; you're now studying it. Every place has at least one (at worst the ground itself: loose stones, a cut bank, a rotten floor; Mechanics); a rich place has two or three (study again for the next). Reach: 30 ft from the feature unless the DM names more (a dam, a powder store, a millrace can reach farther). Setting it off: Utilize action, one precise act from where you stand (or at a distance: a shot, a thrown stone, a tug on a line already rigged); choose one of the named laws. Place a 20-ft Cube, origin within 60 ft; chosen creatures that are in the Cube and within the feature's reach save, DC 15. Fail: 5d8 and the law's effect. Success: half damage only. The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one unless the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest. Mechanics (a load, slope, hanging weight): Dex, Bludgeoning; Restrained (pinned) until the end of its next turn; rubble (Difficult Terrain). Heat (fire, furnace, powder, sealed vessel): Dex, Fire; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn; flammables catch fire. Pneumatics (gas, smoke, foul air): Con, Poison; Poisoned until the end of its next turn; Heavily Obscured until the end of yours. Hydrostatics (water held back): Str, Bludgeoning; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Prone; Difficult Terrain for 1 minute. Optics (strong light and a lens, mirror, or glass of the place): Con, Radiant; Blinded until the end of its next turn.
3Trajectory. Crossbow and firearm attacks ignore Half and Three-Quarters Cover, and long range doesn't give them Disadvantage. From a hole, a wound, or a spent ball or bolt: where the shot came from, about how far off, and what kind of weapon.
3Gunnery. Proficient with siege equipment (ballistae, cannons, mangonels; your DM's rules for siege equipment). When you aim one, it uses your Int for its attack rolls and its attacks ignore Half and Three-Quarters Cover.
3Master Gunner. Looking a gun over tells you its caliber, the shot and powder it takes, its range, and whether it's overloaded, cracked, or badly cast and likely to burst. Ten minutes looking at a wall, gate, or earthwork: roughly how long it would stand against the guns you can see or name, and where a battery would have to stand to breach it. Wherever there are guns you can find paid work as a master gunner, at a wage the DM sets (a skilled hireling's 2 GP a day is a fair start, more in a siege), and gun crews treat you as one of them.
5Out of the Line. Reaction when a creature you can see hits you and you aren't in Medium or Heavy armor: halve that damage (round down).
6Disabling Shot. 3 per Long Rest: when a crossbow or firearm hits the creature you're studying, place the shot instead of dealing Calculated Shot. Until the start of your next turn it has Disadvantage on attacks, or its Speed is 0; or it drops one thing it's holding.
6Fireworks. Given a day, your tools, and 25 GP of black powder and salts: a display of about 10 minutes, seen for miles on a clear night (rockets, wheels, fountains, colored fire); at a feast, a wedding, or a ruler's birthday it gets you noticed by whoever paid. The same work makes up to six signal rockets, each fired as a Utilize action, bursting in a color and pattern you chose, readable by anyone who knows your code.
7Leverage. Your Shove's save DC can use Int in place of Str. Once per round, when you or a visible ally pushes a creature, it goes 10 ft farther. With a lever, rope, or pulley, you lift, drag, or brace as a creature two sizes larger: a portcullis, a loaded cart, a boom, a door four guards are leaning on.
