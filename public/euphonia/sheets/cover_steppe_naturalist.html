Background

Nurbibi is a Central Asian naturalist, a gnome of the steppe's low Hidden folk, and the first to study the steppe's little monsters as animals rather than legends. The griffin legend grew, in our history's argument, out of beaked fossils weathering from the Gobi's red rocks; in Euphonia the beasts are alive, and the steppe has been turning out such creatures for 2,500 years. She grew up on her grandmother's stories and a herding family's knowledge of every pasture, and learned to write things down in a madrasa town. The herders want to know where the beasts will hunt next; a buyer in Bukhara, Moscow, or London wants a live one. The beasts let her closer than any herder, which is how she got nine days in a blind without being eaten.

How to play

  • She learns before she shoots: Natural History tells the table its worst attack, or what it fears, and Field Survey (10th) is ahead. Use what it fears.
  • Take it alive when you can: the stonebow knocks it out at 0 HP instead of killing it, and Cabinet and Specimen make the dead ones worth something too: a remedy against its venom, and a name in the learned world.
  • Bread or Poison keeps the party fed on the steppe, and catches fairy food, which tests as perfect.
  • Gnomish Cunning gives her Advantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saves: a beast's frightening cry or a charm is the herders' problem, not hers. Speak with animals is the naturalist's best instrument: the horse, the eagle, and the griffin-kin's own prey are witnesses.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: a rising figure: the first to study them, not yet the authority. Specimen and Cabinet (6th, a well-made specimen about 50 GP × its Challenge Rating) are what makes her a naturalist and not a hunter; Field Survey (10th) would let her census a valley.
  • Naturalist, as the book sets it: the first to study the griffins' kin as animals. Scribe for Intelligence and the field notes. Forest gnome for speak with animals and darkvision in the blind at night, and because the little monsters let a gnome close.
  • The musket because Ballistics wants a firearm or crossbow and the Dzungars make their own guns; the stonebow from Euphonia's period weapons, for specimens.

Features

Natural Philosopher (Naturalist)
1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.
1Ballistics. Crossbows and firearms (Pistol, Musket, the period firearms; the stonebow is a crossbow) can use Int for attack and damage. Calculated Shot: once per turn, a crossbow or firearm hit on a creature you're studying deals +2d6 of the weapon's damage type. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Muskets: Slow, so a hit cuts the beast's Speed by 10 ft.
1Aim. When you study a creature on your turn, your next attack roll against it this turn has Advantage if it's a crossbow or firearm attack using your Intelligence. Move as you like before or after; restudying the same creature counts, so you can Aim every turn. Breathe out, and let the number settle.
2First Causes. A minute examining something physical, then ask the DM why it happened (or is happening): a sentence or two on the likeliest cause that can be seen, measured, or tested. Too little to go on, the DM says so and the use is kept. Never about minds or feelings. 4 uses per Long Rest.
2Instruments. Expertise with two of your tools, or with one tool and Investigation.
3Mechanism. Finding the feature: study the place (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight) and the DM names one feature you could set off (part of the place: nothing you or your allies placed, lit, or made), the laws it allows, and its reach; you're now studying it. A rich place has two or three (study again for the next); a place with none costs no action. Setting it off: Utilize action, one precise act from where you stand (or at a distance: a shot, a thrown stone, a tug on a line already rigged); choose one of the named laws. Chosen creatures in a 20-ft Cube within its reach, origin within 60 ft, save, DC 15. Fail: 4d8 and the law's effect. Success: half damage only. The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one until the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest. Mechanics (a load, slope, hanging weight): Dex, Bludgeoning; Restrained (pinned) until the end of its next turn; rubble (Difficult Terrain). Heat (fire, furnace, powder, sealed vessel): Dex, Fire; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn; flammables catch fire. Pneumatics (gas, smoke, foul air): Con, Poison; Poisoned until the end of its next turn; Heavily Obscured until the end of yours. Hydrostatics (water held back): Str, Bludgeoning; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Prone; Difficult Terrain for 1 minute. Optics (strong light and a lens, mirror, or glass of the place): Con, Radiant; Blinded until the end of its next turn.
3Natural History. Expertise in Nature or Medicine (proficiency first if needed), and the Herbalism Kit. Studying a creature also gives its habitat and diet, and one thing from its stat block, your choice of kind: its hardest-hitting attack, a limited-use or Recharge ability, or a trait; or instead, what its kind fears or is drawn to (fire, a scent, its young). From a track, nest, spoor, or carcass: what made it, and about when.
3Bread or Poison. A minute examining a plant, fungus, fruit, meat, or water: whether it's safe to eat or drink and, if not, what it would do. You find food and clean water in any land whose plants and animals you've studied for a day, and know which of its people's foods are worth asking about. It catches the Hidden Realm's food too: fairy food tests as perfect, which is how you know.
3Know Its Nature. The creature you're studying makes its save against your Mechanism at Disadvantage (not against Explosion).
5Out of the Line. Reaction when a creature you can see hits you and you aren't in Medium or Heavy armor: halve that damage (round down).
6Specimen. After a Short or Long Rest following the death of a creature you studied, an hour with its remains and your Herbalism Kit or Alchemist's Supplies makes a remedy (one at a time; it spoils at your next Long Rest). Bonus Action, drink it or give it within 5 ft: it ends one poison or disease that kind of creature caused, or for 8 hours grants Resistance to that kind's Poison damage and Advantage on saves against its poisons and diseases.
6Cabinet. An hour with your Herbalism Kit or Alchemist's Supplies preserves the remains of a creature you studied (a skin, a skeleton, a pressed plant, a jar of spirits) indefinitely. You tell a real specimen from a fake at a glance and know what a collector would pay: about 50 GP × its Challenge Rating (minimum 10 GP); an undescribed creature can be worth many times that, at the DM's price, and earns you a name in the learned world.
