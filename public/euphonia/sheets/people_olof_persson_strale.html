In January and February 1658, in the hardest winter anyone remembered, Charles X Gustav marched the Swedish army across the frozen Little Belt and then the Great Belt, island to island, to the gates of Copenhagen; two squadrons of horse went through the ice on the Little Belt and were lost. Denmark gave up Skåne at Roskilde that spring. Resen printed the Edda at Copenhagen in 1665, and the north is reading about the ash with three roots.

On the ice of the Great Belt, the winter the Swedish army marched across the sea, a dragoon who should be dead gets up off the ice. The gray-bearded spirit at his shoulder, a warrior from a hall that has been feasting for a thousand years, is roaring at him to get up, and he does, and roars it in turn at the next man down.

Background

A Västergötland farmer's son of thirty-four who rode with the dragoons across the Belts in 1658 and went down on the ice of the Great Belt with a Danish ball in his chest. He remembers lying there, and the cold, and a gray-bearded man in old mail standing over him, roaring at him to get up because the hall wasn't ready for him. He got up. He has had the old man at his shoulder ever since, loud, opinionated and fond of beer, and he calls him Gubben. Ten years on he is a corporal of dragoons in the regiment's winter quarters and a farmer in summer, and the men of his squadron would follow him through the ice again. The pastor thinks Gubben is a devil; Olof thinks the pastor has never been shot. He wants his son to inherit the farm and not the saddle, and he wants Gubben to tell him, once, what the hall is waiting for.

How to play

  • Your action is a Planar Strike (or a spell); it deals your open vista's plane's damage. Your Bonus Action opens a vista (a Favor from your pool; two for an Influence that heals; never the same plane two turns running) beside a creature within 60 ft, or commands your spirit. The spirit doesn't fight: it acts on your turn, moving and Helping, and commanded it also uses its plane's Aspect.
  • He fights in the front rank with Gubben at his shoulder, and swaps places with him in the press to put himself where he's needed.
  • One More Blow and War Cry make the man beside him fight harder; Rise Each Dawn means the hall still isn't ready for him.
  • The Mead-Bench: any company that drinks together before a fight takes him for one of their own after an evening, and tells him where the fighting is and who pays.
  • The hook is the hall. Gubben knows what it is waiting for and won't say; a party with a reason to ask the dead has a reason to ask Olof.

Why this build

  • Why 8th: ten years since the ice, Valhalla, The Mead-Bench and Rise Each Dawn.
  • The Tree: the Tree's opening vignette, the dragoon on the ice of the Great Belt, and the second row of its calling table (The Classes, Revelator, the Tree).
  • Veteran of the German Wars for a Swedish dragoon of the northern wars; Defense and a cuirass through Tyr's Hand. Broadsword and pistols, the dragoon's arms.
