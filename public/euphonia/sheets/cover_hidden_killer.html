Morfudd Prys

Dwarf · Guide · Ranger 7 (Hidden Killer) · level 7
On the coverThe Welsh border, 1668DwarfA raiding night of the dark half

Every village on every border keeps a Hidden Killer, with iron in the gateposts and salt across the lane. The Wild Hunt rides out of the Bordermarches in the dark half of the year, Samhain to Beltane, hardest at the flood, the bright fortnight around the full moon; in Wales they call its hounds the Cŵn Annwn. Hers is a hill village on the Welsh border above the old mines, and tonight the Hidden are at the flood.

The bell in her hand is the size of a sexton's, and she rings it once at the gate. The mist on the lane doesn't move, and then it does: a white hound, red-eared, that was a patch of fog a moment ago, and behind it riders with no faces she can hold in her eye. The iron nails in the gateposts are warm. She nocks an arrow and says the name she has been saving.

Dwarf

Dwarf (SRD 5.2.1): Medium; Speed 30 ft (40 with Roving); Darkvision 120 ft; Dwarven Resilience (Resistance to Poison, Advantage on saves against Poisoned); Dwarven Toughness (her Hit Points rise by 1 per level, included); Stonecunning (Bonus Action: Tremorsense 60 ft for 10 minutes while on or touching stone, 3 times, her Proficiency Bonus, per Long Rest). The coblynau, the little miners who knock in the Welsh border's old workings, are her people's story: she is one of the low Hidden who took a dwarf's shape to cross over and forgot it was a shape. A Hidden that turns against its own and becomes a Killer is its own business: possible, not special. The village knows what she is and pays her anyway; the new magistrate would call it something else.

Gear

A yew longbow and a quiver of iron-headed arrows (+9, 1d8 + 4 Piercing, Slow; Cold Iron on the first hit each turn); two iron handaxes (+7, 1d6 + 4, Vex, or thrown); a buff coat she wears turned inside out; a staff of rowan as her Druidic Focus, with red thread wound at the head; bread and salt in every pocket; St John's wort dried in her hat; a horn lantern. Languages: Welsh and English (in place of Common and one more), Sylvan and Irish (Deft Explorer). Iron Bell, Uncommon (attuned, Ranger). A small iron handbell, the kind a sexton rings at a funeral. Magic action: until the end of your next turn, each Hidden creature of your choice within 60 ft that can hear it loses the Invisible condition, can't gain it, and can't teleport. Proficiency Bonus rings per Long Rest. Rung at a door, it tells you whether a Hidden creature has crossed that threshold since the last dawn. The Hidden hate church bells; this one hates them back.

Iron and Salt (Adventuring Gear, cheap and common). A bag of horseshoe nails and a sack of salt, renewed every week of the dark half. Ten minutes to iron and salt a room or small building (a great house: 10 minutes a floor or wing) wards every door, window and chimney of it until dawn: a Hidden creature can't pass inward unless someone inside who isn't Hidden invites it (a Charmed householder counts; a lodger's invitation stands while they lodge), or it succeeds on a DC 13 Charisma save, at Disadvantage while the Hidden are at the ebb (not on an open night); one that fails can't enter that place by any warded opening that night. A gap in the iron or a break in the salt ends the ward on that opening.

Her dog. A rough-coated mastiff (as a Mastiff: AC 12, 5 HP, Keen Smell). Not a companion, just a dog. It won't settle when the Hidden are near, and she has learned to watch it more than the mist. Summon Beast is for when a dog isn't enough.

Background

Morfudd Prys is a dwarf of the border's old mines, one of the low Hidden who took the shape and forgot, and now she hunts the Hidden for the village. She is the daughter of the last Killer, who taught her the dog, the milk, the moon and the mist, and was taken on a Samhain night when she was twelve; nobody found a body, which is how she knew. The village pays her in bread, ale and a cottage by the gate, and in a respect that keeps a careful distance: a woman who walks the boundary at dusk and talks to the hedge isn't asked to dinner. The parson and she have an arrangement. He rings the church bell at the open nights; she rings hers at the gate. The new magistrate thinks she is a cunning woman and would like to know her license.

How to play

  • You're a dwarf: darkvision to 120 ft in the dark half, Resistance to Poison, and the toughness to stand at the gate. Stonecunning feels what walks on stone even when the glamour hides it from the eye.
  • The fight you win is the one you saw coming. Read the Tide every evening of the dark half, and when it says something is gathering, ward the gate it will come to, not the place you happen to be.
  • In your ward you go first, nothing hides, nothing teleports, and the charm doesn't take. Drag the fight onto your ground, or, caught off it, run for the gate and throw a ward down where you stand (Nightmare Ward, once a rest: 10 ft around you for a minute).
  • Mark the one wearing the glamour, not the one in front. Cold Iron goes on the first arrow that lands.
  • The bell is for the moment the Hunt goes invisible or starts stepping through the hedge: one ring and they're pinned to the real for a round.
  • Off your ground you are still a Ranger with iron in every shot: Cold Iron works on bandits and wolves too. Out of a fight, you're the village's eyes: the parish map, the dog, Speak with Animals, and a long memory for who went missing when.

Why this build

  • Level 7: a village's Killer, not a legend. Inside Out at 7th is what lets her keep her neighbors from walking into the mist.
  • Guide (Dex +2, Wis +1; Magic Initiate (Druid)): someone who reads country. Dwarf: the coblynau of the border mines, as the setting has it. Archery: the Killer of the subclass's own vignette waits with her bow across her knees.
  • Wales: the brief gives her a village gate, iron and a bell, which is Christian Europe's kit; the Welsh border has the Cŵn Annwn and a word, the March, for exactly where she lives. Any border village in the world keeps a Killer.
  • Dexterity 18 and Wisdom 16 by 4th: +9 with the bow, spell DC 14, AC 16.
