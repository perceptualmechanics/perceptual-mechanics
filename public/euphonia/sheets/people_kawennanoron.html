The Haudenosaunee are matrilineal: a longhouse belongs to a lineage of women, the clan mothers choose and can remove the chiefs, and marriages are arranged by the mothers. The Mohawk are the Keepers of the Eastern Door of the Confederacy, and anyone coming from Albany comes to them first.

The first hearth inside a Mohawk longhouse at the Eastern Door, smoke going up through the roof hole. An older woman ladles corn soup into a bowl for a Dutch trader, a different bowl for a Mahican runner, and a third, smaller and plainer, for a French priest. A girl beside her watches which bowl goes to whom. "Why that one?" the girl asks in Mohawk. "Because he will ask for more," her grandmother says, "and I want him to."

Background

A Mohawk matron of the Wolf Clan, of the Keepers of the Eastern Door, whose longhouse stands nearest the trail from Albany, so every traveler eats her corn soup before anyone else's. She arranges her clan's marriages and has never made a bad one, by her own account. She is teaching her granddaughter which guest gets which bowl, which is to say, everything. She wants a husband for that granddaughter who is a good hunter, a better listener and not, this time, a Frenchman.

  • She is a host, a matchmaker and the gate into the Confederacy from the east.
  • Which bowl you get is her verdict. Earn the fine one.
  • Crew: her clan, which is to say her daughters, their husbands and every young man who hopes to marry into it.
  • The hook is her granddaughter's husband. Bring a good one, or keep a Frenchman away.

Why this build

  • Why 8th: a clan mother of the Eastern Door, whose household is a nation's front step.
  • Majordomo: the longhouse is her household, and every guest is in her book.
