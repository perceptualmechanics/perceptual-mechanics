Doña Catalina de Ibarra

La verdadera destreza, the true skill, is Spain's school of the sword: Jerónimo Sánchez de Carranza founded it in the 1580s on geometry and philosophy, and Luis Pacheco de Narváez, the king's master of arms, made it the kingdom's school. Its students fence inside a circle drawn on the floor, its chords and tangents marking the steps, and the Italians call it witchcraft. In 1668 the king is a boy of six, Carlos II, and the queen mother governs for him.

Madrid, a salle off the Calle Mayor, before the shutters are open. A circle is painted on the boards, with chords and tangents running through it like a geometry lesson. The master stands at its edge and doesn't move. A young lord from Seville, who came to prove the Italian style better, has thrust four times and touched nothing. On the fifth the master takes one step along a chord, and the lord's blade is on the floor. The master bows, and asks whether he'd like to begin his lessons tomorrow.

The manuscript. Her book on the true skill, four hundred pages and ninety figures, every circle drawn with her father's compass. Three printers have offered to publish it under her son's name. She has said no to all three.

The circle. Painted on the salle floor by her husband the year they married and repainted by her every Lent since. The chords are hers now: she moved two of them, and her pupils win more.

Background

The master of a salle off the Calle Mayor, fifty-one, the widow of the man who founded it and the better fencer of the two, which everyone in Madrid now admits and nobody admitted while he lived. She learned the circle from her father, a mathematician who taught the sons of grandees Euclid in the mornings and the sword in the afternoons, and she has read every book on destreza in print and corrected most of them in the margins. The salle's licence is in her son's name, because the guild insists; the pupils know whose it is. Young lords come from Seville and Naples to prove the Italian style better, and stay for lessons. She wants to finish her own book, with the figures drawn true, and see it printed under her own name.

How to play

  • She doesn't move until she has to, and then she takes one step. Economy and the Circle are the whole of destreza: one cut, and wait.
  • A Master's Salle: in Madrid, or at any court where a pupil serves, she can call on one for a sword, a message or a word in the right ear.
  • The Code: she can second a duel, carry a challenge, or tell you whether the other side means to keep the forms. Half of Madrid's affairs of honor pass through her salle.
  • She teaches. A character who takes lessons from her for a season learns where to stand, and what the Italians got wrong.
  • The hook is the book. A printer who will put her name on it, or a patron who will pay for the plates, has a friend with a sword.

Why this build

  • Why 12th: thirty years in the circle and the best blade in Madrid, with Example and A Master's Salle. One Cut (14th) is the stroke she is saving.
  • College of Arms: the Arms college's opening vignette, the master of the Madrid salle who doesn't move (The Classes, Courtier, Arms). The book doesn't say the master is a man.
  • Noble for a mathematician's daughter of good family; Alert because a master sees it coming. Dueling, for the rapier alone.
