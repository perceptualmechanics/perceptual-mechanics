Background

Twenty-six. The younger son of a Royalist colonel killed at Worcester, raised on a promise the Restoration hasn't kept. He came to Whitehall with nothing but a good leg, a better ear, and a talent for lampoons: portraits of types, not people, that everyone in London can sing and everyone is sure the next verse is about the man beside them. One got him banished for a season; he spent it in Paris and came back with better French and worse habits. Five duels, none lost, mostly because he made his opponents laugh. Married to Kit Harrow in the Fleet, on a bet she won; he maintains it doesn't count and has never once tried to prove it.

How to play

  • Out of a fight he's the room: The Read on everyone at the table, Coming and Going to be the only thing anyone looks at or the last thing anyone notices, and The Aside to put one sentence in one ear while Kit plays.
  • In a fight he talks the whole time. Mask first, then fence, and change masks as the fight turns (it's free for the minute); save Ease for Not a Hair when the big one swings. Against a man in a breastplate, the insult is the better thrust.
  • With Kit Harrow: her Big Player puts him where he needs to be, and his Telling Cut is the hit she bets on. Their signals are verse.

Why this build

  • Why 8th: twenty-six, five duels, one banishment, and a reputation older than he is. The Aside (6th) is how he works a room; Satire (10th) is the lampoon that will finally get him killed, and he hasn't written it yet.
  • College of Verse: he gave up the fencing academy for the playhouse, and says so whenever he wins a duel.
  • Noble for Charisma and Skilled; Lucky because he's married to a gambler and it rubs off.
