Ahmed Boşnak

Human · Soldier · Fighter 6 (Battle Master) · level 6
PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornThe siege lines before Candia; the barracks at IstanbulBattle MasterLevel 6

The janissaries are the sultan's standing infantry: boys taken by the devşirme, the levy of Christian children from the Balkan villages, raised Muslim, trained for years and enrolled in an orta, a company with its own barracks, kettle and number. By 1668 the levy is rare and many janissaries are born to it or buy their way in, and the corps keeps shops in Istanbul as often as it keeps watch; but the orta at Candia are the old kind, and the siege has eaten them for twenty years.

A trench before Candia's Sant'Andrea bastion at the change of the watch. A lean man in the tall white börk of the janissaries, its flap down his back, sits on an ammunition chest cleaning a musket lock with a rag and a feather. A boy of the new draft asks him how long he's been here. He thinks about it, holding the lock up to the light. "Since I was nine," he says. "In the corps. Here, four years." He fits the lock back. "Keep your head below the gabions. The man in the third embrasure looks before he fires."

The orta's ladle. Brass, long-handled, older than the siege. The odabaşı who keeps the kettle keeps the company: when the orta turns over its kettle, the sultan has a mutiny. Ahmed's has never been turned over.

His cousin's letters. Six of them, in Bosnian in a Latin hand he never learned to read. He has had one read to him. He carries the rest unopened, because as long as they're unread they can say anything.

Background

An odabaşı, a barracks chief, of a janissary orta, thirty-four, who was taken by the levy from a village in the Bosnian hills at nine and has been in the corps ever since. He remembers his mother's face and the name of his village and almost nothing else, and he has never been back; he sends money to a cousin who writes in a hand he can't read. The orta is his family. He has been at Candia four years and buried half his company there, and the other half would follow him into a mine gallery without asking why. He shoots the best musket in the orta and keeps the kettle, which is the greater honor. He wants the siege over before the new boys die in it, and he wants, once, to go up the valley above Visoko and see if anyone there knows his name.

How to play

  • He is a soldier's soldier: steady, unsentimental and kind to the new draft in ways they won't notice for years.
  • Tactical Assessment and Insight: he reads a trench, a sortie or a man's nerve before anyone moves.
  • In a fight he shoots, cuts and Rallies the man beside him. Archery and Precision Attack make the musket hit.
  • The hook is the valley above Visoko. A party going into Bosnia could carry a question for him, or take him along, and he would owe them more than he could say.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: twenty-five years in the corps, four at Candia; Extra Attack and two Ability Score Improvements.
  • Battle Master on his sheet, for a soldier drilled since boyhood; his Reading starts him as a Champion, the subclass the site's builder offers. Classes in 1668, Fighter: "A janissary has been in the corps since the levy took him as a boy" (The Setting, Characters).
  • Soldier and Savage Attacker for a life in the corps; Archery for the musket. Murad Usta, retired, was in another orta and drinks with him in Istanbul when the corps is home.
