Sir Jasper Kellaway

Human · Noble · Sorcerer 5 (Fire) · level 5
ShowcaseLevel 5Fire OriginVenice, 1668The Venice Job

London still talks about the fire of '66, and every Fire sorcerer in England has been very careful since. The crown's answer is the license: a stipend, a clerk to write down where you sleep, and a gauge on your chest for the clerk to read. Sir Jasper has the license. After the Bankside incident he also has a season abroad, and he has chosen Venice in Ascension week, when the doge marries the sea, the whole city goes masked, and nobody asks a masked man why he is smoking slightly.

The bravo's blade is an inch from Ned's throat when the lanterns along the fondamenta all lean toward the masked Englishman at once. He says, "Oh, damn," in the tone of a man who has spilled wine on a host's carpet, and the bravo's cloak catches, and keeps catching, and the bravo goes into the canal on his own, which everyone agrees was the sensible thing to do.

Background

Sir Jasper Kellaway, baronet, licensed fire sorcerer on a crown stipend. It came on him at sixteen, in the school chapel at Eton, in the middle of the psalm; the chapel survived, mostly. The crown pays him two hundred pounds a year to stay licensed, plus a clerk to write down where he sleeps; he has spent the money and corrupted the clerk. Then came Bankside, which he describes as a misunderstanding with a bear-garden, and the Privy Council's committee suggested Venice for the season. He owes Ned Ashcombe forty guineas and a life, and pays the interest on the second in introductions. Tonight he means to lose the clerk somewhere in Venice and have one evening nobody writes down.

How to play

  • Your Release doesn't hit, it catches: Kindle burns down over three turns (3 dice, 2, 1) unless someone spends an action to put it out. A Kindled enemy has a choice between fighting and not burning, and a burning man can't hide.
  • Wreathed first. It lights the room, punishes whoever closes on you, and makes your Overflow safe for your friends; you have 3 a day.
  • Subtle Spell is the license's loophole: no words, no gestures, and the clerk sees nothing. Suggestion and Charm Person cast that way are why the clerk writes down a lower number.
  • You are a baronet. History and Persuasion open doors your stipend can't, and Lucky (3 a day) covers the rest.
  • Your friends are Ned Ashcombe, Kit Harrow and Moll Saffery, and you owe Ned rather a lot. Stand next to them; don't stand them next to the Fireball.

Why this build

  • Roster: Sir Jasper Kellaway (Europe, The Venice Job), Fire Origin, level 5, as the roster has him. The level fits: Fireball and Fly are his, Burning Bright and the wreathed flight wait for 6th.
  • Noble (Cha +2, Int +1; Skilled) for a baronet with an Eton Latin; Lucky (human Versatile) for a man who keeps surviving his own evenings.
  • Pressure Gauge: the Privy Council's committee pays a clerk to watch him; the gauge gives the clerk something to read, the corrupted clerk something to misreport, and Ned a better chance when it goes off.
  • Subtle and Quickened: the first for the license, the second for the evening it fails.
