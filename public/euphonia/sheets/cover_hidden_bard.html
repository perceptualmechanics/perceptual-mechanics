Tadhg Ó Laoi

Human · Entertainer · Bard 9 (Hidden Bard) · level 9
On the coverConnacht, 1668A hundred years under the hill

Every parish has a fiddler who went under the hill to play for one night's dancing and came home to find a hundred years gone. This one left an Ireland of chiefs and came home to one of transplanted landlords: in the 1650s the English moved the dispossessed Catholic gentry west of the Shannon, into Connacht, onto other people's land. Everyone he knew is dead. The tunes are still his, and so is the debt.

He comes down the boreen in the morning playing, the way he went up it, and the dog at the first farm doesn't know him. The farm isn't the farm. The woman at the door asks in Irish whose he is, and he names his father, and she crosses herself, because his father's name is on a stone in the churchyard that the lichen has nearly covered. He asks what year it is. Then he sits down on the step, and plays her the tune he learned last night, and she sits down beside him and cries for a reason she couldn't tell you.

While he plays it, the first thing he remembers with Remembering after each Long Rest doesn't go on his tally, and creatures that hear it play for a minute or more have Disadvantage on saves against being Charmed by him. He can set it playing on its own for up to an hour: anyone who dances to it doesn't tire, and doesn't notice the time. Use with care. The fiddle doesn't.

Background

A fiddler of a parish in Connacht in the time of the old Queen, good enough to play for a wedding and not good enough to be famous. On the eve of an open night he played at a gathering on the fairy hill for people he didn't know, and played all night, and in the morning a hundred years had gone by. He brought back the fiddle (theirs, not his), a head full of tunes no one ever taught him, the old tongue of the Hidden in his mouth, and a debt he can't remember agreeing to. Now he plays for whoever will feed him, in a country where the families whose halls he played in are gone to Spain or gone under, and the new owners want music too. He is learning the century the way he learns a tune: by ear, and badly at first.

How to play

  • Keep your tally where you can see it: the Due is DC 6 + your Proficiency Bonus + twice the tally, 12 for the first. The fiddle keeps the first one off the tally. Your Performance is +13, so the first three or four are safe.
  • Read the room as the fight starts, and match the season to the creature that matters, not the one in front of you. Cast first, then sing: autumn makes the next save fail.
  • Remembering is your whole toolkit out of a fight: a tongue for an hour (and you need them, a century late), a skill with Expertise, one true sentence about the hill, the stone, the grave.
  • The fiddle can play on its own for an hour, and anyone who dances doesn't tire and doesn't notice the time. You know better than anyone what that does to people. Use it with care.
  • Legend Lore and Geas are what you brought back: the old stories are true, and the Hidden's bargains bind.
  • Remember what nobody at the table can do: the tongue nobody speaks, the lock nobody can pick, the old story nobody knows. A skill your friend already has is their moment, not yours.

Why this build

  • Level 9: the gift was a large one (a Rare fiddle, a head of tunes); level 5 spells and four Expertises make him a master who doesn't know the century, not a beginner.
  • Entertainer (Cha +2, Dex +1; Musician) and Lucky (human Versatile): a working fiddler, and the man who came back.
  • Expertise in Performance first, because the Due is the subclass: +13 at 9th. Into a Place and Remember a Spell (6th) are his; the Teind, which sends the bill to an enemy, and Turn the Season, which leaves a season as a creature's temper for an hour (both 14th), aren't yet.
  • Connacht: the subclass's own vignette is a Connacht shebeen, and a hundred years puts him on either side of the Cromwellian transplantation, which is the sharpest homecoming the century offers. A table can move him (a Telemark fiddler the fossegrim taught is the other vignette).
