PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornÜsküdarFactorLevel 7

A waqf is a pious endowment in Islamic law: property given forever to a charitable purpose (a fountain, a soup kitchen, a school, poor brides' dowries) and run by a trustee, the mütevelli. Ottoman women of means founded a great many of them, and Üsküdar, on the Asian shore of Istanbul, is full of their fountains.

A bench by a marble fountain in Üsküdar, its water cold even in August. An old woman in a fine plain cloak goes through a wedding dress with a young man in a trustee's turban, pricing the lace aloud, and the young man winces at each figure. She turns to you and asks, quite pleasantly, whether you know anything about ledgers. The young man winces harder.

Background

A widow of Üsküdar who left her husband's fortune to a waqf that dresses poor brides, keeps her street's fountain cold with ice in summer and takes the quarter's orphans to the spring meadows once a year. She sits on the bench by the fountain she paid for and goes through every bride's dress with the trustee, pricing the lace aloud. The trustee is her nephew, and he has started to borrow from the waqf. She wants it to outlive him.

How to play

  • She is a patron for any job that ends in a wedding, and many that start with one.
  • Follow the Money: she knows where every coin of her waqf went, and she is beginning to know where her nephew's went.
  • She doesn't fight. She buys the fight off, or makes it embarrassing.
  • The hook is her nephew and the ledger. Help her keep the waqf whole, and every bride in Üsküdar is in your debt.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: a fortune given away wisely, and a widow who still counts every coin of it.
  • Factor: a waqf is a house, and she runs its books.
  • Noble for her husband's house; Lucky, because she says so.
