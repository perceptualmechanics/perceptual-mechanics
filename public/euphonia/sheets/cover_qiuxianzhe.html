Lu Qingxu

Human · Sage · Wizard 9 (Diviner) · level 9
On the coverQing China, 1668The Qiu Xian Zhe

The Qiu Xian Zhe are the fallen court alchemists. They lost their honors in 1567; in the Red Pill Case of 1620 an emperor died the morning after taking mineral pills; after 1644 they withdrew to the hills. 1668 is a low point. They believe the old imperial tombs hold the recipes their predecessors were disgraced for, and 1668 is a cheap year to dig, with the new dynasty watching rebels, not graves.

Under the hill, the tomb's last door is sealed with a talisman older than the dynasty that buried the prince. He reads it by lamplight, twice, and writes his own beneath it in cinnabar. The door opens. Behind it, something that has been waiting since the Ming sat up when the paper went on, and he raises the lamp to see whether it's the elixir or the reason no one came back.

His hold Sanctuary, Protection from Evil and Good, and (the Uncommon one) Remove Curse. He wrote them himself, in the Bureau's old hand.

His teacher's last pill. A red pill in a black lacquer box, from the batch his teacher took the night he died. It may be the elixir. It may be what killed him. He has never opened the box, and he has never left it behind.

Background

Trained in the old way, as the last of a line of court alchemists who served the Ming until the Ming stopped wanting them. His teacher died of an elixir of his own making, and he still doesn't know whether the batch was wrong or the man was. When Beijing fell he went to the hills in Daoist dress, which keeps the queue off his head and the questions off his past. The Astronomical Bureau chose the day each Ming prince was sealed in; he worked beside the men who chose them, and he remembers which dates didn't add up. Now he digs. The Qing are watching the rebels, and nobody is watching the graves.

How to play

  • You're the one who knows how tombs work. Read the sealing date, the talisman, the geomancy of the valley: History with Expertise, Legend Lore, Locate Object for the vessel that matters.
  • Divinations pay for themselves: every Divination spell cast with a level 2+ slot hands back a lower one. Scout with Arcane Eye and Clairvoyance before anyone opens a door.
  • Portent is two numbers you already know. Spend the low one on the guardian's save and the high one on the friend at the trap.
  • Talisman papers are spells anyone can use. Hand them out before the tomb, not after.
  • Your teacher's pill is a story, not a feature. Whether you ever take it is your call, and the DM's.

Why this build

  • Diviner: the fangshi's arts were astrology, the calendar, the choice of days and the siting of tombs, as much as elixirs; the Diviner is the 2024 subclass for knowing what is behind the door. The setting's Geomancer is the Hermetic copy of the fae's space and time, and doesn't fit an elixir-seeker as well; there is no 2024 Transmuter.
  • Level 9: a court alchemist's real learning (Scrying, Legend Lore) with no court, no Bureau and no patron. Fallen, not green.
  • Sage (Int +2, Con +1; Magic Initiate (Wizard)) for a court scholar; Crafter (human Versatile) for the alchemist's supplies and the mason's tools that open a sealed door.
  • Intelligence 20 by 8th; Constitution 16 for a wizard who goes underground.
