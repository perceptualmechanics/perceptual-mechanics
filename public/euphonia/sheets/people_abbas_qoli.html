A naqqal tells the Shahnameh, Ferdowsi's Book of Kings, in coffeehouses, acting every part with a stick for a sword and a voice for every hero, and breaking off at the best moment so the audience returns. Isfahan's coffeehouses around the Maidan are where the city talks.

A coffeehouse off the Maidan after the evening prayer, every bench full. A grey-bearded man on a stool, a stick in his hand, is Rostam, then Sohrab, then Rostam again, and when the father learns whom he has killed, a room of grown men is weeping into their coffee. He stops there. The room groans. He holds out his cap for coppers and refuses, absolutely, to say what happens tomorrow.

Background

A naqqal of Isfahan who tells the Shahnameh in a coffeehouse off the Maidan, with a stick for a sword, and can make a room of grown men cry over Sohrab. He sits two tables from Farrokh of Isfahan, and they have been rivals for thirty years and friends for twenty-nine. Everything a coffeehouse says, he hears. His daughter Parvaneh tells it better than he does, to the women of the quarter on the roofs on summer nights, and what he wants is one hall where they can both tell it.

How to play

  • He is a contact who hears everything a coffeehouse says, and the best way to spend an evening in Isfahan.
  • The Aside lets him say a great deal to one listener in a crowded room. He will use it for the party, or on them.
  • He and Farrokh are a double act: rivals on their stools, friends after closing.
  • The hook is Parvaneh and the hall. Find a hall where a woman can tell the Shahnameh to men and women both, and he will owe you his best story.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: thirty years on the stool, and the city's best Sohrab.
  • College of Verse: the naqqal is the stage, the voice and the sword in one man.
  • Entertainer and Musician for the coffeehouse; Skilled for what he hears there.
