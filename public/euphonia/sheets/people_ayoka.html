Oríkì are the praise poems of Oyo's families and people: names, deeds and epithets chanted to a lineage or a person, and remembered for generations. The chanter knows every family's glories, and the parts it leaves out.

A festival day in Akinwale's quarter of Oyo Ile, drums everywhere. A handsome woman in an indigo wrapper chants each family as it passes, louder for the ones who tip. You are a stranger, and she greets you with a line of praise for your grandfather, made up on the spot, which is already better than anything he did. The drummers pick it up. By evening children in the next compound are chanting it.

Background

A chanter of oríkì. For thirty years she has composed everyone else's praise. She knows who was born to whom in every compound of the quarter, which debts are owed and which marriages were made in a hurry, and she is the most discreet person in Oyo until a praise poem needs a sharp line. She has added a line about laughing to Akinwale's oríkì; the drummers have picked it up, and he pretends not to have heard it. She trades what she knows for a good story she hasn't heard, and she wants, at last, a line in her own oríkì worth chanting.

How to play

  • She is a contact first. Every family in the quarter is in her head, and she trades it for a story she hasn't heard. Bring her one.
  • In a fight she stands behind the drummers and makes the enemy forget its lines: Cutting Words and Vicious Mockery.
  • Speak with Dead and Augury are the ancestors listening to their oríkì. A praise-singer can ask the dead what the living left out.
  • Give her a deed worth a line. The party that does becomes part of her repertoire, and Oyo will know them for a generation.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: thirty years of other people's praise, and Magical Discoveries: she has learned what the ancestors answer.
  • College of Lore: the griot's college, a court's memory that knows every family's debts.
  • Entertainer and Musician for a life at festivals; Skilled for the medicine and the reading of people a chanter picks up in thirty years of compounds.
