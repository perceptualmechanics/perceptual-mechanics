Telemark's fiddlers say the fossegrim, who plays under waterfalls, will teach the fiddle to anyone who leaves him a smoked leg of mutton or a white kid on a Thursday evening and doesn't look back; a lean offering teaches only how to tune the strings. The Hardanger fiddle, with sympathetic strings under the bowed ones, was already being made in western Norway by the 1650s. Norway in 1668 is ruled from Copenhagen.

Below a waterfall in Telemark, an hour after dusk on a Thursday. A young man in a sheepskin jerkin sets a smoked leg of mutton on a flat stone at the edge of the spray, turns around and walks away, very carefully not looking back. Behind him the falls play a slått he has never heard. By the time he reaches the farm he can play it, and four more, and his fingers are bleeding a little, and he has the feeling that he said yes to something.

Background

A farmhand of upper Telemark, eighteen, with a borrowed Hardanger fiddle and no prospects, until he left a smoked leg of mutton for the fossegrim below the falls and didn't look back. He came home playing slåtter nobody had taught him. At the first wedding he played, the guests danced until the third morning and begged him, laughing, to let them sit down; he couldn't stop either, until the bride's grandmother took the bow out of his hand. The valley engages him for every wedding now, and keeps an old woman by him with instructions. Somewhere in that night under the falls he made a promise. He doesn't remember what it was. He has been back to ask, and the fossegrim played him the answer, and he can't remember that either. He would like to know before it comes due.

How to play

  • Read the room first, and match the season to the creature that matters.
  • Remembering is a farmhand's whole education: a tongue for an hour, a skill with Expertise for one check, one true sentence about the falls. Each goes on the tally, and the Due climbs.
  • Encouraging Song: after a rest, a slått gives Heroic Inspiration to his friends. At a wedding, it gives it to the whole room, and nobody can sit down.
  • Play him as eighteen: delighted, alarmed, and very good at something he didn't earn.
  • The hook is the promise. He is asking everyone who has been under the hill and come back, and a party that has may be next.

Why this build

  • Why 3rd: the summer he left the mutton. Everything the Hidden gave him, and none of the bill paid yet.
  • Hidden Bard: the Hidden Bard chapter's second opening vignette, the boy below the Telemark waterfall (Life on the Border, Hidden Bard).
  • Entertainer and Musician for a wedding fiddler; Lucky because he didn't look back.
