Johanka Vlková

Human · Sage · Wizard 8 (Geomancer) · level 8
ShowcaseLevel 8GeomancerPrague, 1668The Children of the Emerald Tablet

Rudolf II kept Dee and Kelley at Prague, and in Euphonia Dee's stones worked. Sixty years after the emperor, his Royal Garden behind the castle is overgrown and his collections are scattered, and the Children of the Emerald Tablet, the Hermetic order that calls the Geomancer's whole discipline copying the fae's homework, read the treatises anyway. Now a Kelley-era manuscript has surfaced in a Jesuit library, with an account of the first shewstone in it, and everyone who studies figures in Prague wants to read it first.

In the Royal Garden at dusk she paces out a square in the long grass that nobody else can see, drops to her knees at its corner, and draws four rows of dots in the dust with her finger. The Jesuit with her asks, as they all do, whether she is some fortune teller. "Tonight they come over the Stag Moat," she says, "from the north, about an hour after moonrise. Stand where I put you."

Mason's Square and Compasses (Wondrous Item, Uncommon, requires attunement by a Geomancer Wizard). Brass, worn bright at the joint. While she holds them she can inscribe her Figures one more time before she rests (counted on the sheet), and measure any distance she can see to the inch. Once per dawn, 10 minutes with them lays out a building, road or camp so its lines are true: for 24 hours it can't be thin ground, and nothing teleports in or out except through a door. A Prague lodge-master's, pawned in the war and never redeemed. She paid the pawnbroker's widow twice what it was worth, and would again.

A sliver of black glass. Her great-grandfather swept floors for Rudolf's court when Dee and Kelley were in Prague, and swept this up from under their table. It might be from the black mirror. It might be from a wine glass. She keeps it in a twist of paper in her bodice, and has never once looked into it.

Background

Born in the Malá Strana below the castle, to a family that served Rudolf's court in small ways; her great-grandfather swept the floors of the room where Dee and Kelley worked. She learned figures from an old man who claimed to have known Kepler's assistants and lied about everything but the math, and then from the gardens themselves, where the fae's chalk marks still turn up on the old paths after a bad night. The Children's masters ask her what she is, some fortune teller, and then quietly ask her where to dig. She would trade them nothing. What she wants is the manuscript in the Clementinum, and its account of the first shewstone, before the Children get it, because she thinks she knows which of the garden's paths it was found on.

How to play

  • Figures are your Bonus Action every turn: six of them, back on a Short Rest, no rolls for distance or damage. Cast with your action, draw with your Bonus Action.
  • The Square knocks one creature down and 10 ft sideways toward whichever edge you name. Name the edge with the drop, the river or the fire behind it. On a friend, it walks them up the wall and across the roofs for a minute.
  • The Circle holds one creature for a minute: put it around whatever wants to reach your wizard's body, or around the one trying to run with the manuscript. Then Square it: inside your Circle it doesn't fall, it's pinned Prone where it stands, and the Circle holds.
  • You always know the hour, the height and which way is down, and Squaring the Map finds the room the plan says isn't there. In Prague, that is half the job.
  • Shop Talk: masons, surveyors and astronomers take you in anywhere. And yes, you can cast a sand chart for a client. It does nothing. Clients rarely notice.

Why this build

  • Roster: Johanka Vlková (Europe), Geomancer, level 8, as the roster has her: Square and Circle, and Squaring the Map for a woman who finds what is buried.
  • Sage (Int +2, Con +1; Magic Initiate (Wizard)) for the scholar; Alert (human Versatile) because a Geomancer's first Figure is best drawn before anyone moves.
  • Mason's Square and Compasses: the subclass's own item, one more Figure (six), and true lines that keep a camp from being thin ground, which in the old emperor's garden is worth having.
  • Intelligence 20 by 8th: DC 16, and five Figures before the Square.
