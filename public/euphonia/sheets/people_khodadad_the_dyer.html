When the Safavid court astrologers declared Shah Safi II's first coronation ill-starred, he was crowned again in 1667 as Shah Suleiman. In Euphonia the bad stars had to go somewhere: a Zoroastrian dyer was set on the throne for an afternoon to take them. The Gamester's luck is his trade now, whether he likes it or not.

A dye yard in Isfahan's Zoroastrian quarter, vats of indigo and madder steaming. A heavy, cheerful man stands knee-deep in a blue vat, arms blue to the elbow, and waves at you, hoping you're a customer. When you tell him who sent you, he sits down in the dye. A pigeon lands on the rim of the vat, looks at him, and falls in.

Background

The Zoroastrian dyer who was set on the throne before the second coronation to take the bad stars off the shah. It worked, and as far as anyone can tell he still has them: roofs fall in near him, dice go strange around him, and he has learned to call the odds before anything happens, because something always does. He wants to be forgotten, or failing that, for his daughter's wedding to go ahead with nothing going wrong at it. One court faction wants him safe and quiet; another wants him back on the throne for an afternoon.

How to play

  • He is the heart of the substitute (see Hooks): comic, kind and dangerous to stand near.
  • Bad luck follows him. The DM can spend it: once a scene, something goes wrong nearby, and he called it.
  • He is a Gamester because he has to be. He would trade all of it for a quiet afternoon.
  • The hook is his daughter's wedding. Make it go off with nothing going wrong, and the whole quarter will dance for the party.

Why this build

  • Why 3rd: a dyer, not an adventurer: the Gamester's gift arrived with the stars, last year.
  • Gamester: he calls the odds because the odds now call on him.
  • Artisan and Crafter for the dye yard; Lucky, for a man who hasn't been since the coronation.

1Exploit. When a creature you can see hits an Observed creature you can see (no action; after the hit, before damage): +2d4 of the attack's type, not doubled on a Critical Hit. Not before your first turn in a combat; once used, not again until the start of your next turn.

2Direct. Once per turn when you Observe on your turn: an ally within 60 ft who can see or hear you adds your computation die (d4) to its next attack roll or ability check against an Observed creature before your next turn (to hit it, to talk it round, to read it, or to fool it), deciding after the d20.

2Deduction. 10 minutes of stillness, then ask the DM one question about a creature, object, place, or event you've Observed or seen, or whose evidence you've examined: the most likely truth that follows, in a sentence or two. It moves the case forward without closing it; the DM can hold the answer to the end of the scene or your next rest (the use isn't spent until answered). Not enough data: the DM says so, no use spent. Never thoughts or feelings; false data gives an answer built on it. No roll. 3 uses per Long Rest.
2Mental Index. Expertise in two skills you're proficient in.

3Where the Action Is. In any settlement, within an hour you find where the serious play is, who keeps the bank, and who's welcome. You learn any game by watching it for 10 minutes, and in downtime the tables cover your food and lodging.
