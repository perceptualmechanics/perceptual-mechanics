Saatçi Mehmed Ağa

Human · Retainer · Counsellor 11 (Marshal) · level 11
PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornThe siege camp before CandiaMarshalLevel 11

The Ottomans landed on Crete in 1645 and laid siege to Candia (Heraklion) in 1648; in 1668 it is the longest siege anyone has known, and the Grand Vizier, Köprülü Fazıl Ahmed Pasha, has been in the lines himself since 1667, pushing the mines and the trenches closer every month. Venice holds the city with mercenaries, volunteers and French gentlemen who come for a season of glory and go home with dysentery.

The Ottoman lines before Candia, in the twenty-first year of the siege. A man in a plain gray kaftan sits on a camp stool under a parasol his servant holds steady, and the Venetian sharpshooters on the bastion stopped wasting powder on him a week ago: nothing they send arrives where he is. "The third embrasure," he tells the archer kneeling beside him. "He reloads to his right, and he looks before he fires. Count four." The archer counts four.

His is a plain ebony rod the length of a forearm, notched for counting; the gunners watch it and not him.

The notebooks. Seventeen of them, one for each bastion and outwork, every shot from every embrasure entered with the time. The Venetian engineers would give a great deal to read the one marked Sant'Andrea. So would some of the pashas.

Background

A staff officer of the Grand Vizier's household, sent to the Candia lines to tell the pashas what the walls are going to do. The camp calls him Saatçi, the watchmaker, because he counts everything: shots from each embrasure, the minutes between them, the loaves going into the trenches and the men coming back out. He has a notebook for every bastion, and he has outlived four pashas' opinions of him. He was a page in the palace school as a boy, where they taught him to write a fair hand and to keep his face still; he kept both. The Venetian sharpshooters have given up on him, and the janissaries put money on how close the next ball will come. He wants the siege to end in his lifetime, and then a garden in Üsküdar with a sundial he built himself.

How to play

  • He tells the people beside him when to shoot, and they hit. Observe, Direct, Spotter and Exploit make an ordinary soldier the best shot on the line.
  • Siegecraft: ten minutes looking at a wall and he knows its garrison, its stores and its weakest point. Inside the walls he knows how the besieger will come.
  • Staff Officer and Logistics: he can count a camp, a fleet or a caravan in ten minutes, and tell you the day it runs out of bread.
  • Sidestep, with no limit: shot does not arrive where he is. He finds this less remarkable than everyone else does.
  • The hook is the end of the siege. Someone who can carry a message across the lines, or get a look inside the Sant'Andrea bastion, is someone he can use, and he pays in information.

Why this build

  • Why 11th: two decades of sieges, Faster Than Thought and Siegecraft, and Cold Read. Fire Control (14th) would make every gunner in the trench his.
  • Marshal: the Counsellor chapter's opening vignette, the man in the gray kaftan before Candia (The Classes, Counsellor). Kâtib Halil Efendi keeps the same camp's commissary; this is a different man.
  • Retainer, for a palace page who became the Grand Vizier's staff; two Skilled feats for a man who has had to know a little of everything.
