Wat Ellery

Human · Border-Born · Ranger 8 (Mortal Hunter) · level 8
ShowcaseLevel 8Mortal HunterThe Bordermarches, 1668Herefordshire-born

The Mortal Hunter is the Hunt, and the Hidden Killer's opposite number. It rides the tide, strikes where the Hidden and the real run together, and doesn't kill what it hunts: it takes it. Its arrow strikes the way sudden sickness strikes, and what falls is led away down a road only the Hunt can see. Most of the Hunt's hunters were taken themselves, once.

The dark half, the moon three days from full. A light in the mist on the low field that looks like a lantern in a cottage window, and a voice from behind it calling the shepherd's boy by the name only his mother uses. The boy stops to catch his breath. Beside him, a young man in a jerkin his grandfather might have worn, a flint-headed arrow on the string, not drawing it, and an oak bucket chained at his hip.

Background

Wat Ellery was nine, in old King Henry's time, the smith's boy in a Herefordshire village, when he followed a light across the water-meadow. He has hunted for the Hunt ever since, to take rather than to kill, because that was how he was taken; he looks about thirty and has stopped counting. He runs behind Étaín of the Ford with her bucket, and twice he has thrown it over her when she was already turning toward him; both times she thanked him in a language he didn't know he understood. He took the last Killer of Morfudd Prys's village on a Samhain night, knows where the last Killer is now, and has never told anyone; he spoke once across a ford to the parson there, Hywel Bowen, and didn't tell him either. What he wants is to see his mother's village once in daylight, and not be recognized.

How to play

  • Shoot first, take second. Elf-Shot is your damage and it softens the prey; Take the one who would hurt the Hunt most, not the one about to fall to the next arrow anyway.
  • The ground is half the fight. On thin ground your prey saves at Disadvantage and comes back only when someone calls its name where it vanished; call the mist when there's none. A Hidden Killer's ward is never thin ground, and the mist can't be called into one, so don't fight on hers.
  • Watch the tide. At the flood you get a Take back every Short Rest. On an open night, what you take you can keep, up to a year and a day; tell the DM before you try it on someone the story needs.
  • A Hidden Domain priest can call the taken back unless you took them on thin ground; a Faebreaker can hold on. Take the ones nobody is holding, or win the contest (your spell attack against their Athletics).
  • You're a Hidden creature. Iron and salt keep you out of a warded house, the Killer's Cold Iron finds you, and the parson knows you on sight. You can't get lost, you're hard to track, and the Hunt's road is yours from 11th.
  • Someone carries the water. When Étaín goes deep, you're within 5 ft with the bucket.

Why this build

  • Level 8, as the roster has him: Pixie-Led, the light on the moor that leads the blow astray. A century and more of hunting, but a boy of the Hunt, not one of its lords.
  • Border-Born (Dex +2, Wis +1; Alert): the setting's own background, for a child of the border village who heard the Hunt coming and still followed the light. Magic Initiate (Wizard) (human Versatile): the lantern in the mist, as illusion.
  • Wisdom over Dexterity: Take is his one button and runs off his spell save DC, so the level 8 Improvement goes to Wisdom (18, DC 15), with Dexterity 18 for +9 with the bow. Expertise in Survival (+10) was for the old Holding On contest; since 2026-11-10 the Taken contest uses his spell attack (+7), so it's tracking now.
  • Flint, not iron: elf-shot is the flint arrowhead the plow turns up. The Elf-Arrow he wears is the one that took him; worn, it brings the wearer home if Taken, which hasn't helped him.
