Hekim Yahya Efendi

PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornIstanbulThe SüleymaniyeDoctorLevel 7

Süleyman the Magnificent's mosque complex on the hill above the Golden Horn has, beside the mosque, a hospital (darüşşifa) and a medical madrasa, where physicians have taught on the wards since the 1550s. The Islamic world's teaching hospitals are the oldest there are (The Classes, Physician): Lanternhall in Basel is barely a dozen years old. The Kadızadeli preachers of Istanbul suspect anything new, and some of them suspect anything old as well.

The long ward of the Süleymaniye's hospital at the morning round, the light coming in high under the domes. An old physician walks the beds with his hands behind his back and a student on either side. At the fourth bed he stops and asks the patient, not the students, what the trouble is, and listens to the whole answer. Then he lays two fingers on the man's wrist, and a thread of light runs from the wrist to the woman in the next bed and to the boy in the bed past her, and all three of them breathe at once.

Background

The senior physician of the Süleymaniye's hospital, who has taught at the bedside for twenty years as his teachers did and theirs before them. He heals with the Font as calmly as he prescribes a syrup of violets, because he learned both the same way, by study and at the bedside, and he asks no one's leave for either. Lanternhall's letters, full of excitement about the Font, he reads with a grandfather's patience and answers with corrections. He writes to Hakim Ishak Toledano at Edirne every week; they agree on almost nothing and have never missed a week. The young preachers ask whether healing like his is permitted. He answers that God sent no disease without its cure, and goes back to his round. He wants to finish his commentary on Ibn Sina's Canon in Turkish, so his students can stop borrowing his Arabic.

How to play

  • Link the party before the day starts, with one Bonus Action, and your healing reaches anyone in the chain from where you stand.
  • Vitals: every time you heal, you read another patient. In a fight that tells you who is about to fall; at a reception it tells you who is ill and hiding it.
  • The Title and the Practice: "the doctor" is let in to see the patient before anyone asks for papers, and after a day's practice in a place the DM answers two questions about who is sick, beaten, hiding or dying.
  • He teaches. Ask him a question and he answers it with three better questions, kindly, and then the answer.
  • The hook is the preachers. A party that needs a healer in Istanbul nobody will denounce this month will need to know who is preaching against him, and why.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: twenty years on the ward, House Call and the Practice, and Vitals: he reads the next patient while his hands are still on this one.
  • Doctor: the Pitch's "practitioner of healing magics in the halls of Constantinople" (The Setting, Welcome to Euphonia, The Pitch); the book calls the city Istanbul everywhere else, and so does he.
  • Schooled, for the Süleymaniye's madrasas; Magic Initiate (Cleric) for a prayer said over the sick. Intelligence 18 drives the chain.
