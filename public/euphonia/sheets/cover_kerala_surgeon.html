The leaf pattern. The craft's own trick: a leaf cut to the shape of the part to be rebuilt, laid on the skin to mark the flap. With a pattern and an hour, a rebuilt nose takes; the scar is the price, and it fades.

Inoculation, the old way. He knows the variolation India's healers already practice (the Inoculation Lancet in Euphonia.md): the slow way's immunity that magic can't give.

Background

A surgeon of a Kerala family whose craft is the nose rebuilt from a flap of the patient's own skin, cut to a leaf's pattern and turned down from the forehead or the cheek: a repair said to be old, traced to Sushruta, though who practices it in 1668 is uncertain (the setting marks it so). The south has given him work: in living memory Mysore's general cut off civilians' noses and Madurai answered in kind, and the noseless come to him from both sides. His family keeps its knowledge in palm-leaf books, as Kerala's physicians do. In Euphonia his knife floats, and it is still the same craft.

How to play

  • He works from across the room: the knife goes where he sends it, and the healing goes with the cut.
  • Out of a fight he is a surgeon: Treatment on the patient within 10 ft of the hovering knife; the knife can work a Healer's Kit and he can stabilize or examine through it; and the old repairs magic can't make look right.
  • Seal the table with Resilient Sphere when the work can't be interrupted; nothing gets in, and he can still heal and treat inside it.

Why this build

  • Why 10th: a master of a hereditary craft, mid-career. The Sealed Table (10th) is the sealed table; Theatre Closed (14th) is ahead.
  • Surgeon, not Chirurgeon: the instrument that never touches the patient suits a craft of fine, slow cuts. Artisan for a family craft, and Crafter because he forges his own blades.
  • Healer by Versatile, because a craft family teaches the dressing before the knife.
