Gear

A bao board of dark wood, carved by his grandfather, with seeds polished by four generations, a dagger in his sash (+7, 1d4 + 4; Nick), a set of honest dice and a set of others, a pack of Portuguese cards, a white kanzu and an embroidered cap, a ledger of what everyone owes him written in Swahili in Arabic script. Languages: Swahili, Arabic, Portuguese, Gujarati. Tools: Gaming Set (bao), Gaming Set (dice), Gaming Set (playing cards), Navigator's Tools (Merchant).

The bao board. Four rows of eight pits, his grandfather's carving. He has won a dhow on it, lost a house on it and won the house back. He doesn't lend it.

The captain's cargo. Forty bales of Gujarat cotton in his cousin's warehouse, waiting for the captain to come back and play for them. He usually does.

Background

A Mombasa man of thirty-eight from an old Swahili family that has had less money every generation since the Portuguese came, who keeps the family's stone house on the waterfront in repair with bao. He plays in the shade below the fort every afternoon, against anyone, for anything: a cargo, a horse, a favor, a letter of introduction. He never cheats at bao, because he doesn't need to; at dice he is less particular, and the Portuguese garrison has learned not to play him at either. He gives back what he wins from men who can't afford it, quietly, through their wives. He wants to play the best bao player on the coast, who lives in Pate and won't come south, and he wants his sister's son to stop trying to learn from him.

How to play

  • He is a contact who knows every captain in the harbor by his tell. Everyone's a Mark: one evening of bao and he knows how a man bets for a week.
  • Where the Action Is: in any port, within the hour, he knows where the serious play is, and who keeps the bank.
  • Honest Assessment: lay a plan out for him and he tells you the odds and the biggest risk, kindly.
  • In a fight, Know the Odds tells the party exactly how much a foe has left, and the Count makes the blow that finishes it.
  • The hook is the player in Pate. A party sailing north could carry a challenge, or bring the man south.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: the best player in Mombasa, with Big Player, Know the Odds and Everyone's a Mark. Stacked Deck (10th) is for the day he plays Pate.
  • Gamester: the Gamester's opening vignette, the quiet man in a Swahili cap who wins a cargo at bao (The Classes, Counsellor, Gamester).
  • Merchant for an old trading family; Alert because he always moves first, at the board or anywhere.
