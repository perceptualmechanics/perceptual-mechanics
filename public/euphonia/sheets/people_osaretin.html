Benin's brass plaques on the palace pillars record the court's history in metal, cast by the oba's guild and nobody else. Olfert Dapper's description of Africa, with its picture of the palace, is newly printed in Amsterdam in 1668.

The casters' street in Benin City in the heat of a pour, the furnace roaring. You may watch from the doorway, and not one step further. A thickset man with burn scars to the elbows lifts the crucible himself. Afterward he wipes his hands, looks at where each of you is standing, and tells you who among you is in charge, who thinks they are, and who is going to argue about it later. Then he asks how they draw a plaque in Amsterdam, and with what.

Background

A master of the oba's guild of brass-casters. The oba keeps to his palace and his chiefs use the power he doesn't, and the plaques on the palace pillars record all of it, cast by Osaretin's guild and nobody else's. He can read a room's ranks from where people stand and cast them from memory a week later. A Dutch factor has told him that Europe's ministers of magic are reading about Benin's palace in a new book from Amsterdam, and he would very much like to know what they think it does. He wants to see Dapper's book with his own eyes and find out what the Dutch got wrong.

How to play

  • He reads rank from where people stand: The Read and Every Table make him the person to ask who really matters in a room at the oba's court.
  • A plaque of his is a door into any house that owns one, and every chief's house owns one.
  • In a fight he lights the enemy for his friends and paints a wall across the street when the guard comes. A caster's arms swing an eben well.
  • The hook is the book. A party that carries Dapper's volume from Amsterdam to Benin has a friend at court, and an audience for whatever the Dutch got wrong.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: a master of the guild, not its head. Trompe-l'oeil and Anamorphosis (6th) are a craftsman whose work tricks the eye.
  • College of Art: brass, not paint, but the eye is the same. Smith's Tools are his instrument.
  • Noble: the guild of brass-casters is a palace society with titles of its own. Crafter for the wax, the clay and the bronze.
