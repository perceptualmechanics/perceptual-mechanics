Kilimoar

Human · Acolyte · Druid 13 (Shapers) · level 13
ShowcaseLevel 13Circle of ShapersPohnpei, 1668The Monsoon Road

Nan Madol, the basalt city on Pohnpei's reef, is the plainest proof that the world can be asked: walls of stone columns laid crosswise like logs, some heavier than any crew could lift, which Pohnpei says the builders flew into place. The samworo, Pohnpei's priests, keep the rites there and keep the asking. Traders off the Monsoon Road would give a cargo for the method, and the island's chiefs would like their sea walls built with it.

He has been sitting cross-legged on the platform since the rain started, eyes shut, one hand flat on a fallen column as long as a canoe. The Mutapan with the notebook has been waiting three hours. Then the old man laughs, pats the stone twice, and says, "All right." The column rolls over once, like a sleeper, and lifts, and walks itself up onto the course where it was always meant to lie, and the Mutapan drops his pencil in the sea.

Gear

A hibiscus-fiber kilt, a turmeric-dyed sash, and a woven sleeping mat; a staff of mangrove hardwood as his Druidic Focus; a conch-shell trumpet; a bundle of sakau root wrapped in leaves, for offerings at the old platforms; a basket of breadfruit paste and dried fish; a flake of basalt from the first column that ever answered him, sixty years ago, worn smooth in his palm. Languages: Pohnpeian (in place of Common), the speech of the Carolinian navigators, enough of the Monsoon Road's trade tongue to refuse an offer, and Druidic.

The pei at Nan Madol. A forgotten offering platform on the city's eastern edge that the chiefs no longer visit and he does. Out of a fight he works there: Raise the Works seven days running would make anything he wished permanent from 14th, and he has decided, for now, not to.

Background

A samworo of Pohnpei, of the old teachers' school at Salapwuk in the island's interior, sixty-eight years old and the slowest-moving man on the island by choice. He made Sehpin listen to the basalt for a year before letting them touch it, and has never regretted the year. The stones answer him slower than they did, and he has decided, privately, that it is the stones that are tired and not him. The Nahnmwarki would like sea walls; the Monsoon Road traders would like the method; a Mutapan Mhizha has come all the way from the capital to see whether it can be bought. He laughs at all of them. What he wants is the rites at Nan Madol kept, after him, by someone who will not sell them, and he is beginning to suspect that someone is Sehpin and that Sehpin will be asked to choose.

How to play

  • Your Wild Shape uses are your power budget, and the field is your canvas. Cast with your action; shape with your Bonus Action. You can hold two shapings at once.
  • Rampart before the archers fire, Pillar to put a friend on the high ground (or a foe off it), Grasp to hold the brute where your fighter wants it.
  • Keep one use, or the free one, for The Wall Comes Up: a Reaction that puts stone in front of whoever was just hit, and can turn the hit into a miss. After round one, your Bonus Action reworks a shaping for free: keep the canvas moving.
  • Out of a fight you are the island's engineer, and its priest. The Mason's Hand, Raise the Works and Move Earth build; Commune with Nature, Divination and Augury ask first. You always ask first.
  • You can still take a beast's shape; the uses are one pool. You haven't in forty years. Some say once, to see what the shark thought of the wall.

Why this build

  • Roster: Kilimoar (Oceania), Circle of Shapers, level 13, as the roster has him: Living Stone (two shapings, walls that heal), Wall of Stone, and level 7 spells. What Stands at 14th is the city's own lesson, and he has not yet chosen to make anything permanent.
  • Acolyte (Wis +2, Int +1; Magic Initiate (Cleric)) for a priest of the samworo; Skilled (human Versatile) for the chants, the genealogies and the country.
  • Magician and Potent Spellcasting: he fights with spells and stone, not a spear; War Caster keeps Wall of Stone standing when the shot comes.
  • Wisdom 20 by 12th: DC 18 on the Pillar and the Grasp. No armor: a sixty-eight-year-old priest in the rain; his ramparts are his cover.
