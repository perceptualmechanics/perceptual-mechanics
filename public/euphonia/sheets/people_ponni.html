Ayyanar is the guardian of the Tamil village boundary, and his terracotta horses stand at his shrines so that he and his attendants can ride the boundary at night. The potter-priests who make the horses are the nearest thing the Tamil country has to Hidden Killers.

The Ayyanar shrine outside a village near Madurai on a dark night, a hundred clay horses pale in the starlight. A girl of seventeen walks the boundary with a sickle and a clay lamp, talking to the horses as if they were her brothers. One of them answers. She doesn't stop walking. Behind her, very quietly, something big and invisible keeps pace, its hooves making no sound on the dust.

Background

A potter-priest's daughter who helps make Ayyanar's terracotta horses and has started riding out behind them at the boundary. Her father would rather she were the nearest thing to a potter. She has better hands for clay than either of her brothers and a better seat on a horse that isn't there. She is seventeen and quietly furious about almost everything. She wants her father to let her make one horse that is hers alone, from clay to firing.

How to play

  • She is just starting out: one arrow a turn, a ward, and a great deal of nerve.
  • Whatever rides behind Ayyanar's horses at night rides behind her now too. Let the party see the hoofprints in the morning.
  • She is furious with her father, her brothers and the boundary, and polite to the horses. Play the fury quiet.
  • The hook is her own horse. A party that helps her make it, from clay to firing, will find out what rides it.

Why this build

  • Why 3rd: seventeen, and just begun. Cold Iron and Prepare the Ground are new to her.
  • Artisan and Crafter: a potter's daughter, and a better potter than her brothers.
  • Archery for the boundary, where you want to see a thing before it reaches you.
