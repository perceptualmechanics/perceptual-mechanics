Tseren Dorji

Human · Noble · Fighter 9 (Battle Master) · level 9
PeopleThe Steppe and the NorthThe Ili valley, DzungariaBattle MasterLevel 9

The Dzungar khanate is the rising steppe power of the 1660s. Sengge rules as khan; his half-brothers resent him, and his brother Galdan is a monk in Tibet. In our history the half-brothers murder Sengge in 1670 and Galdan comes home to take his place.

The khan's tent in the Ili valley, felt walls, a feast. A half-brother of the khan raises a cup and gives a long toast to brotherhood. Beside the door a zaisang stands with his hand never far from his saber, and while everyone else listens to the toast he counts the men by the door, and the men outside it, and the horses saddled at this hour of the night.

The letter. To the monk Galdan, in Tibet, asking him to come home. It needs to arrive with an argument the monk's teachers will accept. He has not found the argument.

A Dzungar musket, spare. Payment for whoever carries the letter. The Dzungar armorers make the best guns on the steppe.

Background

A zaisang, a Dzungar noble official. He has watched Sengge's half-brothers count their men and wants Sengge's brother home from Tibet before they move. He needs someone to carry a letter, and an argument, to teachers in Tibet who can't be ordered, and he pays in good Dzungar guns. He wants the monk home and the khan alive, in that order if it can't be both.

How to play

  • He is a patron with a dangerous errand: carry a letter to Tibet, and make the argument when it arrives.
  • Tactical Assessment and Know Your Enemy: he counts men the way others breathe.
  • In a fight, a mounted officer: two saber cuts and an order.
  • The hook is 1670. He knows what the half-brothers mean to do. The table may know too.

Why this build

  • Why 9th: a zaisang of standing, with Indomitable and Tactical Master.
  • Battle Master, as the roster has him: a soldier who reads the room before the toast.
  • Noble and Skilled for a Dzungar official; Mounted Combatant for a man who has lived in the saddle.

Maneuvers

Tactical Assessment. Add a Superiority Die to a History, Insight or Investigation check: counting the men by the door. (Player's Handbook)

Precision Attack. Add a Superiority Die to an attack roll, before or after it, before the result. (Player's Handbook)

Commander's Strike. Forgo one attack and a Bonus Action: an ally makes a weapon attack with its Reaction, adding the die to damage. (Player's Handbook)

Rally. Bonus Action: an ally gains Temporary Hit Points equal to a Superiority Die + half your Fighter level. (Player's Handbook)

Parry. Reaction when hit in melee: reduce the damage by a Superiority Die + your Strength or Dexterity modifier. (Player's Handbook)
