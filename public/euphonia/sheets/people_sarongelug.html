The Carolinian wayfinders navigate by the star compass, the swells and the birds, and imagine the canoe still and the islands moving past it. The schools keep their knowledge within the school. The Natural Philosopher's Navigator is Euphonia's nearest class: the laws the world already obeys, read by a master.

A voyaging canoe a day out from Polowat, no land in sight. An old navigator lies in the bottom of the hull with his eyes shut, reading the swells through his back. "Pikelot," he says, naming an island over the horizon, and adjusts nothing, because the canoe is already pointed at it. The Dutch passenger checks his chart, finds the island, and stops checking his chart.

Background

A palu, a master navigator of the Carolinian wayfinding schools on Polowat, who holds the star compass and the swells in his head. He imagines the canoe still and the islands moving past it, and arrives. A Dutch captain offered him a year's silver to pilot a passage no chart shows, and he told the captain, kindly, that the school doesn't sell and the captain couldn't pay. He will take passengers, if he likes them, and teach them nothing but how to keep quiet in a good wind. He wants to see his granddaughter's son finish his training, and to make one more voyage to Guam before the Spanish change it.

How to play

  • At sea he cannot be lost: Plotted Course, Taking the Fix without instruments, Weather Eye for the next day's wind.
  • Pilot: on any ship bound his way he works his passage, with five companions fed and carried. He is the party's way across the ocean, if he likes them.
  • He teaches nothing to outsiders but how to keep quiet in a good wind. A party that keeps quiet learns more than it expects.
  • The hook is Guam: the Spanish arrive there this year. One more voyage, before it changes.

Why this build

  • Why 10th: a palu, a master of his school. Pilot and Through the Gap (10th) are the canoe and the crew moving as one.
  • Navigator, as the roster has him: the laws the sea obeys, read without a chart.
  • Sailor and Tavern Brawler: a life in canoe houses. Intelligence 18 and Wisdom 16 by 8th.

Features

Natural Philosopher (Navigator)
1Natural Philosophy. Study (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight): a creature, object, or feature you can see within 60 ft; no check. One creature plus one object or feature at a time; studying another replaces the earlier one of its kind. A creature stays studied 1 minute; an object or feature until you finish a Short or Long Rest. Learn one fact. A creature: its AC, which save has the lowest bonus, one Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability (the DM chooses; or tells you it has none), or its Speeds and special senses. An object or feature: what it's made of, rough age, how it was made, or where it's weakest. Studying what you're already studying renews it and teaches another fact. You can study the place itself (from level 3, that finds Mechanism's features). Only what can be seen, heard, or measured; never thoughts or feelings. Measure: by eye and instrument, a thing's distance, height, weight, and temperature to within a tenth, and the time of day to within a few minutes.
1Ballistics. Crossbows and firearms (Pistol, Musket, the period firearms; the stonebow is a crossbow) can use Int for attack and damage. Calculated Shot: once per turn, a crossbow or firearm hit on a creature you're studying deals +3d8 of the weapon's damage type. Weapon Mastery: the mastery property of one kind of crossbow or firearm you're proficient with, changed on a Long Rest: Hand Crossbows (his stonebow counts as one): Vex.
1Aim. When you study a creature on your turn, your next attack roll against it this turn has Advantage if it's a crossbow or firearm attack using your Intelligence. Move as you like before or after; restudying the same creature counts, so you can Aim every turn. Breathe out, and let the number settle.
2First Causes. A minute examining something physical, then ask the DM why it happened (or is happening): a sentence or two on the likeliest cause that can be seen, measured, or tested. Too little to go on, the DM says so and the use is kept. Never about minds or feelings. 4 uses per Long Rest.
2Instruments. Expertise with two of your tools, or with one tool and Investigation.
3Mechanism. Finding the feature: study the place (Bonus Action, or 1 minute outside a fight) and the DM names one feature you could set off (part of the place: nothing you or your allies placed, lit, or made), the laws it allows, and its reach; you're now studying it. Every place has at least one (at worst the ground itself: loose stones, a cut bank, a rotten floor; Mechanics); a rich place has two or three (study again for the next). Reach: 30 ft from the feature unless the DM names more (a dam, a powder store, a millrace can reach farther). Setting it off: Utilize action, one precise act from where you stand (or at a distance: a shot, a thrown stone, a tug on a line already rigged); choose one of the named laws. Chosen creatures in a 20-ft Cube within its reach, origin within 60 ft, save, DC 16. Fail: 5d8 and the law's effect. Success: half damage only. The feature is spent; what it leaves isn't a new one until the DM says so. 5 uses per Long Rest. Mechanics (a load, slope, hanging weight): Dex, Bludgeoning; Restrained (pinned) until the end of its next turn; rubble (Difficult Terrain). Heat (fire, furnace, powder, sealed vessel): Dex, Fire; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn; flammables catch fire. Pneumatics (gas, smoke, foul air): Con, Poison; Poisoned until the end of its next turn; Heavily Obscured until the end of yours. Hydrostatics (water held back): Str, Bludgeoning; pushed 10 ft from the feature and Prone; Difficult Terrain for 1 minute. Optics (strong light and a lens, mirror, or glass of the place): Con, Radiant; Blinded until the end of its next turn.
3Plotted Course. Navigator's Tools (another tool if you have them). You and your companions can't get lost except by magic, you always know north, and a fast pace costs the group no Perception. On any plane.
3Taking the Fix. With Navigator's Tools and a clear sky, 10 minutes fixes your latitude to a few miles and the local time to the minute; on land, with a telescope and a clear night, your longitude too. An hour and a day's travel make a chart of the way you came that others can follow; you read anyone's chart and know how far to trust it.
3Course Correction. Bonus Action: call a path. Up to 4 allies within 30 ft who see or hear you can each use a Reaction to move half their Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks. Course Correction and Reading the Currents share one supply: 4 uses (Int modifier, minimum once) per Long Rest.
3Reading the Currents. Reaction, from the supply you share with Course Correction: when a creature you can see hits an ally within 30 ft, halve that damage to the ally (round down).
5Out of the Line. Reaction when a creature you can see hits you and you aren't in Medium or Heavy armor: halve that damage (round down).
6Safe Passage. Reaction, after the roll but before you know the result: a creature within 30 ft adds +4 to a Dex save against an effect you can see.
6Drift. Speed +10 ft (included); Difficult Terrain costs you no extra movement.
6Weather Eye. You predict the weather where you are for 24 hours, and any storm, fog, or change of wind coming within that time; near the sea, the tides; on a monsoon coast, whether the season is about to turn.
7Leverage. Your Shove's save DC can use Int in place of Str. Once per round, when you or a visible ally pushes a creature, it goes 10 ft farther. With a lever, rope, or pulley, you lift, drag, or brace as a creature two sizes larger: a portcullis, a loaded cart, a boom, a door four guards are leaning on.
9Correspondence. One question by letter to the Republic of Letters (history, nature, a place, a creature, an object). Someone knows: a reply in 1d6 days with as much as they know, which the DM decides. No one knows: a letter says so. The learned can be wrong. Then not again for 7 days.
10Through the Gap. Course Correction also moves you up to your Speed, and everyone who moves can pass through hostile creatures' spaces (not end there); the movement provokes Opportunity Attacks as usual.
10Pilot. Given an hour to watch the water, you bring a ship safely into any harbor or through any strait you can see. On any ship bound your way, you work your passage as pilot, with up to five companions fed and carried.
