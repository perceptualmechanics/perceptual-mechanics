Kurokawa Tadashige

Human · Noble · Fighter 9 (Battle Master) · level 9
PeopleEast and Southeast AsiaSendai, the Date domain's castle townBattle MasterLevel 9

Japan has been at peace since the siege of Osaka in 1615, broken only by the Shimabara rising of 1637–38. A samurai of 1668 is a salaried official who wears two swords and may never draw them; every domain keeps sword instructors, and the schools multiply as the wars recede, teaching discipline, posture and the mind as much as the cut.

A dōjō in Sendai in the gray before dawn, the floor cold. An old man in a plain hakama watches twenty young samurai of the Date household swing their wooden swords in unison, and says nothing for a long time. Then he walks down the line, stops behind one boy, and moves the boy's left heel half an inch with his own foot. The boy's next cut is the best he has ever made. "Again," the old man says. "You will never need it. Do it as if you will."

The notes. Forty-one forms of the school, each written out in his own calligraphy with a drawing and a single line of commentary. The line for the last form says only: "When you understand the first, you will not need this one."

The moss garden. Behind his house, raked every morning. He says it teaches more than the dōjō. His pupils think he is joking. He is not.

Background

The sword instructor of a Date retainers' school in Sendai, sixty-eight, who rode to the siege of Osaka with the Date army as a boy of fifteen, the last great battle anyone in the castle town remembers, and has taught the sword in peace for fifty years since. His pupils are the sons of officials who will spend their lives keeping accounts and will never draw in earnest, and he teaches them as if every one of them will. He is courteous, exact and very funny in private. He keeps a garden of moss that he is prouder of than of any cut. He has outlived his wife, his school's founder and two daimyo, and his son keeps the household's books. He wants one pupil who will understand what he is teaching, which is not the sword, and he wants to see the sea at Matsushima once more in the autumn.

How to play

  • He is a mentor first: a character who studies with him for a season learns where to stand, and why it matters when it doesn't.
  • In a fight he is stillness, then two cuts: Disarming Attack and Riposte make most opponents stop wanting to fight him.
  • Tactical Assessment: he reads a stance across a room, and knows what a man will do before the man does.
  • The hook is the pupil who understands. A party with a young fighter who has something to learn will find him, or he will find them.

Why this build

  • Why 9th: fifty years of teaching and one battle, Indomitable and Tactical Master.
  • Battle Master on his sheet, for a teacher of forms; his Reading starts him as a Champion, the subclass the site's builder offers. Classes in 1668, Fighter: "A samurai with fifty years of peace behind him teaches fencing to men who will never draw" (The Setting, Characters).
  • Noble and Skilled for a samurai of the Date household; Dueling for the katana alone.

Maneuvers

Precision Attack. Add a Superiority Die to an attack roll, before or after it, before the result. (Player's Handbook)

Disarming Attack. On a hit, add the die to damage; the target makes a Str save or drops what it holds. (Player's Handbook)

Parry. Reaction when hit in melee: reduce the damage by a Superiority Die + your Strength or Dexterity modifier. (Player's Handbook)

Riposte. Reaction when a creature misses you in melee: attack it, adding the die to damage. (Player's Handbook)

Tactical Assessment. Add a Superiority Die to a History, Insight or Investigation check: reading a pupil's stance before he moves. (Player's Handbook)
