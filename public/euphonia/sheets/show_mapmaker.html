Ramji Parekh

A Gujarati Mhizha whose family paid the guild to train him a generation ago. He was the first foreigner the workshops took, and the reason they stopped. Struck from the rolls, he teaches anyone in Sofala who can pay, and draws charts of the coast that agree with each other to a hair, which the pilots buy and the guild calls theft.

A rented godown behind the Sofala quay, after dark. A plump, cheerful man in white cotton has a map spread on a crate, and the map is drawing the room as the captain walks in: the crate, the lamp, the captain's own feet. "Your lamps will be ready by the morning tide," he says. "No stamp. And this," tapping the margin, where a line of ink is still crawling, "is the rock off Bazaruto your pilot doesn't know about. That part is extra."

Background

Ramji Parekh's family trades on the Monsoon Road out of Surat and got tired of paying Mutapan hands to retune its cargo, so it paid one, once, to teach him instead. He was the best apprentice in his year. The guild entered him on its rolls, and then, when it understood what it had done, struck him off. He set up in Sofala with a godown and a pupil, then six, and half the unlisted Mhizha on the coast learned from him; he is proud of every one of them. He wants his name back on the rolls, which he will never admit, and his daughter married into a good Surat house, which he admits constantly.

How to play

  • Contour Lines is your opener: Prone and rooted in place for a turn, everyone in the circle, for two of your five folds. Then let the melee have them.
  • Fold Space costs one fold, for anyone, not only you. Pull the cornered ally out, or put the fighter where the archer is. Spend the five as the day needs: two Contour Lines and a Fold, or five Folds.
  • Out of a fight you are a map. You can't get lost; Survey finds the secret doors and traps; Route tells a caravan the safest way and the fastest; Fair Copy copies any chart and spots a lie in one.
  • Retuning (Tinker) is your living: a hold of drifting curiosities retuned overnight, no stamp, no questions. The guild knows, and can't prove it.
  • Lucky's three points and a Flare are your defense. You're a teacher, not a soldier: stay behind someone.

Why this build

  • Level 8, as the roster has him: a master in all but the rolls. Survey and Route (6th) are the chartmaker the pilots pay; Flare and Field Workshop (7th) are the old hand. Wide Fold and Longitude (10th) are ahead, and Longitude is the secret the guild most fears he'll sell.
  • Merchant (Int +2, Cha +1; Lucky) for a man who was a trader before he was anything. Crafter (human Versatile) for the discount and the hands: weaving is the family's other trade.
  • Intelligence 20 by 8th; studded leather (AC 13), because he expects to be behind someone.
  • He is Gujarati, of a Surat merchant house: the Monsoon Road's Gujarati traders were long established at Sofala and up the coast by 1668.
