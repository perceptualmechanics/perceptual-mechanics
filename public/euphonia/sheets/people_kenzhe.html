Every raid of the Wild Hunt has a Mortal Hunter riding ahead, who takes and doesn't kill. On the steppe the Hunt rides out of the pari's country in the dark half.

Just past the camp's watch fires, the dark half of the year. A rider sits his horse at the edge of the light, smiling his old smile, and calls the watch by their childhood names: the boy who was afraid of the dark, the girl who stole his apricots. One of the watch takes a step toward him before his sister's hand catches his collar.

His old smile. Not an item: the thing the watch sees past the firelight. He calls them by their childhood names, and some of them answer.

A bowl at the feast. The camp still sets one for him. He empties it. Sometimes he leaves something in it: a feather, a ring, a child's lost shoe.

Background

Aisulu's brother, a Kazakh rider taken by the pari three winters ago, who came back riding ahead of the Wild Hunt and takes people rather than killing them. He isn't cruel: he takes people the way you'd carry a friend out of a burning yurt, and he truly believes they are happier there. The camp still sets a bowl for him at feasts, and something empties it. He wants to bring his sister across, because he truly believes she would be happier there.

How to play

  • He is a villain who loves the people he takes. Play the warmth; it is real.
  • He knocks out, he doesn't kill: Elf-Shot and Take. The camp wakes up one rider short.
  • Pixie-Led: he cannot be tracked, and neither can whoever rides with him.
  • The hook is his sister. The meeting between Aisulu and Kenzhe is the story of the steppe in the dark half.

Why this build

  • Why 9th: three winters with the Hunt, and its best rider.
  • Mortal Hunter, as the roster has him: the rider ahead of the Hunt who takes and doesn't kill.
  • Guide for a rider of the steppe; Archery for the horn bow.
