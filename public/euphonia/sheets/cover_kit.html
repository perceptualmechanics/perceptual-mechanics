Background

Twenty-four. A Cavalier baronet's daughter: her father came home with the king in 1660 and lost the estate at hazard at the Groom Porter's inside two years. She learned the game to win it back, found she was better at it than anyone she'd ever met, and never stopped. She does the season like a campaign: Whitehall in winter, Tunbridge Wells in summer, Paris when London gets too hot, the Bankside bear garden when she's bored, in a borrowed coat and a borrowed accent. She owes one moneylender in Lombard Street, who is very patient and very frightening. Married to Ned Ashcombe in the Fleet, on a bet she won; she has the register copy.

How to play

  • Call Ned's thrust before he rolls: a hit with his Telling Cut behind it takes her extra die too. Calling and missing only costs her the round, so call the ones she likes the odds on.
  • Out of a fight: Honest Assessment (a Deduction) on any plan Ned has (he has many), and Everyone's a Mark on anyone at the table worth an evening.
  • With Ned Ashcombe: his couplets are her signals, Know the Odds tells him how much is left to cut, and her Big Player is his exit. All In (14th) is the bet she hasn't made yet.

Why this build

  • Why 8th: twenty-four, and already banned from the Groom Porter's for a season. Big Player, Know the Odds, and Everyone's a Mark (6th) are her whole act; Your Own Chair (10th) is the chair she means to win back, and Stacked Deck (10th) the night she can't lose.
  • Noble for Intelligence and Skilled; Lucky, because of course.

1Exploit. When a creature you can see hits an Observed creature you can see (no action; after the hit, before damage): +2d8 of the attack's type, not doubled on a Critical Hit. Not before your first turn in a combat; once used, not again until the start of your next turn.
1Sidestep. Reaction, when a creature you can see hits you: halve the damage (round down), then move up to 10 ft without provoking Opportunity Attacks. Sidestep and Anticipate share their uses, which have no limit from 5th.
2Direct. Once per turn when you Observe on your turn: an ally within 60 ft who can see or hear you adds your computation die (d8) to its next attack roll or ability check against an Observed creature before your next turn (to hit it, to talk it round, to read it, or to fool it), deciding after the d20.
2Anticipate. Reaction, when an Observed creature you can see hits you or a creature within 30 ft: subtract d8 from the attack roll, possibly turning the hit into a miss. On you, Sidestep takes the sure half and Anticipate can erase the hit; on an ally, Anticipate is the one you have.
2Deduction. 10 minutes of stillness, then ask the DM one question about a creature, object, place, or event you've Observed or seen, or whose evidence you've examined: the most likely truth that follows, in a sentence or two. It moves the case forward without closing it; the DM can hold the answer to the end of the scene or your next rest (the use isn't spent until answered). Not enough data: the DM says so, no use spent. Never thoughts or feelings; false data gives an answer built on it. No roll. 5 uses per Long Rest.
2Mental Index. Expertise in two skills you're proficient in.
3Card Sharp. One Gaming Set and Sleight of Hand. At a game whose set you're proficient with: Int for any check it calls for, and Advantage to cheat or to catch a cheat.
3Where the Action Is. In any settlement, within an hour you find where the serious play is, who keeps the bank, and who's welcome. You learn any game by watching it for 10 minutes, and in downtime the tables cover your food and lodging.
3Honest Assessment. A use of Deduction on a plan (heard laid out, or with its key people Observed): likely, even, or unlikely, and its single biggest risk. Proven right to a patron: Advantage on Charisma checks to influence it until an assessment you give it proves wrong.
3The Count. Once per round, when a creature is about to make an attack roll against an Observed creature and you could Exploit, call it before the roll (no action). Hit: you Exploit it and roll one more die (3d8). Miss: no Exploit until the start of your next turn.
4Read About It. 3 times per Short or Long Rest: add your Proficiency Bonus to a check with a skill or tool you aren't proficient in (decide before rolling).
5Wider Gaze. Observe reaches 120 ft and learns two facts (three within 10 ft). Sidestep and Anticipate no longer have a limit on uses.
6Big Player. Bonus Action, 3 per Short or Long Rest: an ally within 60 ft who sees your signal can take a Reaction to move up to its Speed, provoking no Opportunity Attacks from Observed creatures.
6Know the Odds. Observe also tells you the creature's AC and current Hit Points. Tell the table as much as your character would.
6Everyone's a Mark. An evening at play with a creature: whether it bluffs, how much it can afford to lose, and its tell; Advantage on Insight against it for a week.
7Agile Nerves. Dex saves for half damage: none on a success, half on a failure (not while Incapacitated).
7Outdo. Checks with a skill or tool you have Expertise in: a d20 roll of 9 or lower counts as 10.
