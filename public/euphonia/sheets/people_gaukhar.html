PeopleThe Steppe and the NorthThe autumn pastureCollege of VerseLevel 6

An aqyn is a Kazakh improvising poet-singer, and an aitys is a contest between two of them, verse against verse to the dombra, often a man against a woman, before a crowd that decides the winner. At a wedding the bride is sung out of her mother's yurt with a lament, the synsu, and welcomed into her new family with the betashar.

A feast on the autumn pasture, a dozen yurts, the whole valley come to watch. A young woman with a dombra across her knee answers a challenger's verse before he has finished it, rhyming on his own last word and turning it on him. His uncle laughs first. Then everyone does. The challenger, scarlet, laughs too, and asks for another round. She plays the first notes before he has finished asking.

The list. Not written down: every unmarried young person in the valley, who is watching whom, and which families would object. She is usually right.

Background

A Kazakh aqyn from Aisulu's camp, whom no man on the autumn pasture has beaten in an aitys in six years. She sings the bride out of her mother's yurt at every wedding in the valley, and arranges a fair number of them, because the singer at a wedding hears which young people are watching each other. She wants one rival worth the name before she's too famous for anyone to try.

How to play

  • She is an ally and a matchmaker: the singer at every wedding knows who is watching whom.
  • The Aside and The Cutting Line are an aitys in a sentence. A party that crosses her will be in a verse by sundown.
  • Challenge her to an aitys and lose well, and she will be your friend for life.
  • The hook is the rival. Find her one worth the name, and the whole valley will come to watch.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: six years unbeaten on the autumn pasture.
  • College of Verse: the aitys is verse as a duel.
  • Entertainer and Musician for the dombra; Lucky, because nobody wins six years running on talent alone.
