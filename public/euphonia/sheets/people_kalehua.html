Kalehua

The Makahiki is the Hawaiian season of peace and harvest in honor of Lono, from about October to February, when war is forbidden and the people hold games: surfing, boxing, sled-racing down grass slopes and spear contests. Surfing, heʻe nalu, is a chiefly art, and a famous surfer has chants composed in her honor, or composes them.

Dawn on a beach at Kona, the swell coming in long and glassy. A tall young woman with a board taller than she is sits on the sand, reading the waves and refusing to say whether today's the day. Two chiefs' sons are waiting for her answer. She stands, runs, and is out past the break before either of them has his board. When she comes in on the biggest wave of the morning, she is already chanting a verse about how slow they were.

Background

A chief's daughter of Kona on Hawaiʻi Island who has won the Makahiki surfing three years running and composes the chants that tease whoever she beats. She has beaten two chiefs' sons and been forgiven by one; the other is still composing his reply. The sea likes her, the old women say, and when she rages the water seems to rise with her. She wants a match against someone worth beating before she marries.

How to play

  • She is an ally and the chief's daughter in the hook, if the table wants: the best surfer on the island, and the sharpest tongue.
  • Challenge her to anything, and lose well. She remembers good losers and puts them in her chants.
  • In a fight she is fast and fearless, and Rage of the Wilds makes everyone beside her braver.
  • The hook is the match. Find her someone worth beating before she marries, or be that someone.

Why this build

  • Why 5th: three years the Makahiki champion, and a temper the sea seems to share.
  • Path of the Wild Heart: the sea and its creatures answer her.
  • Entertainer and Musician for the chants; Lucky, because you don't win three years running without the sea's favor.
