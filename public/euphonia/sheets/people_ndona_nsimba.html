A nganga in Kongo heals, divines, guards against the ndoki (those who harm by witchcraft) and makes minkisi, the bundles and figures that hold a power and seal an oath. In Euphonia the oath bundles work, and the Capuchins burn them when they can.

The edge of the sacred wood of Nfinda Ngula at dusk. A broad, laughing woman with white chalk on her brow binds an oath bundle in cloth and cord, an nkisi under her other arm like a market basket, while a Dutch trader waits with a fever in his face. She names her price for the cure. He argues. She laughs, and puts the price up.

Background

A nganga of Soyo. She knows the wood of Nfinda Ngula the way a scholar knows a library: what grows there, what lives there and what is owed. She makes the oath minkisi that seal the count's treaties, and lords who break an oath sworn on one of her bundles have been known to sicken. She and the Capuchin heal the same fevers in the same streets under an arrangement neither will admit to; she thinks him a decent man badly taught and tells him so, in Kikongo, which he is learning. She wants him to stop burning her work and start asking what it's for.

How to play

  • She heals for a price and laughs while she haggles. Nobody in Soyo leaves her compound sick if they paid; nobody leaves it cheated, either.
  • Oaths are her real power: Geas and the oath minkisi. A party that swears on one of her bundles should keep its word.
  • She is a teacher for any Druid or Cleric who will listen, and a quiet ally against the Capuchins' fires.
  • The hook is the friar. Get him to ask her what an nkisi is for, and she will owe the party a favor; get him to burn one more, and she will want to know who told him where it was.

Why this build

  • Why 9th: a nganga the count trusts with his treaties. Geas and Greater Restoration are a lifetime in the wood.
  • Circle of the Land, as the roster has her, with the Tropical land: she knows the wood of Nfinda Ngula as a library.
  • Hermit and Healer for the healing; Crafter for the bundles, which are made with the hands as much as the prayers.
