Samuel van Hoogstraten of Dordrecht, a pupil of Rembrandt, made perspective boxes in the 1650s: a painted room inside a wooden case, seen through a peephole, so true that the eye walks down a corridor the box is too small to hold. Dordrecht is the oldest city in Holland and the home of the Grand Pensionary, Johan de Witt; its regents buy paintings the way other cities' lords buy horses.

Dordrecht, a merchant's hall. The box is two feet wide. The courtier beside it is watching the guests' faces.

The box with the extra room. Her fourth box: a Dordrecht kitchen, a corridor, a courtyard. Look long enough down the corridor and there is a door at the end she never painted, standing a little open. A regent's widow bought it, and gave it back a month later without saying why.

The sketchbook. Two hundred faces at the peephole, each with one word under it: honest, merchant, watch. A few say something else. One says Mutapa.

Background

A glass-grinder's daughter of thirty-three who paints, grinds lenses and builds perspective boxes of her own in the manner of Hoogstraten's, and who stands beside the regent's box at every reception in his hall because she has learned more from people's faces at the peephole than from anything they have ever said to her. The ones who gasp are honest. The ones who look for the trick are merchants. The ones who look a long time and say nothing are the ones to watch. Her own boxes sell to the regents for the price of a good horse, and one of them, she says, has a room in it she didn't paint. She wants to build a box no one can tell from a real room, and then she wants to know who is living in the one she didn't paint.

How to play

  • She reads a room from its faces: The Read, Every Table and her sketchbook make her the person to ask who in Dordrecht is honest, who is a merchant and who to watch.
  • The Likeness: an hour with her brush and a sitter can't hide from her in any disguise, and she knows if the sitter dies.
  • In a fight she paints it: a wall across the alley, a door through the wall behind the party, the foe lit so nothing hides it.
  • Anamorphosis: her letters and sketches read as landscapes to anyone she didn't mean them for.
  • The hook is the box with the extra room. Someone is living behind the door she didn't paint, and the regent's widow knows who.

Why this build

  • Why 7th: Trompe-l'oeil and Anamorphosis (6th) and Quick Study; a painter at the height of her craft, not yet famous.
  • College of Art: the Art college's opening vignette, the courtier beside Hoogstraten's peepshow box in Dordrecht (The Classes, Courtier, Art). She isn't Hoogstraten's pupil; she learned from his boxes.
  • Artisan for a glass-grinder's daughter; Glassblower's Tools for the lenses, so What the Eye Can See adds Carpenter's for the cases.
