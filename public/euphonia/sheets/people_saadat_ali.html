In India the pari of the Persian stories keep their weddings too, and a mortal who eats at the feast is married to the Realm. The Dreamlord is the Hidden Realm itself; its gift to Saadat was a wedding present.

A lane near the Jamuna in Agra at midnight. A young man in a slightly too splendid coat sits on the doorstep of his bride's family's house, plucking a rabab, a tune he never learned. Every dog in the lane has come to lie down and listen. Upstairs, a shutter opens an inch, and closes again. He keeps playing.

The invitations. Leaves that arrive on the wind, written in a hand that is not ink. Each names a night. He has not gone. Yet.

Background

An Agra bridegroom who went to a pari wedding, came back after what he swears was one night, and found it had been three years and he was married twice. His bride's family wants him home; the paris consider him married, and so, he admits, does he. The Hidden Realm gave him a little of its power as a wedding present and keeps sending invitations. He sleeps badly and plays beautifully. He wants to be forgiven by his human bride, and he hasn't told her he'd go back.

How to play

  • Comic relief, and sincere about it. He loves both his brides and cannot fix it.
  • Step into the Hidden gets him, and a friend, out of most trouble. Manners tells him when a pari bargain is binding, which is usually.
  • In a fight he would rather not be there. Sleep and Suggestion first.
  • The hook is the next invitation. When he goes, someone should go with him.

Why this build

  • Why 4th: back from the wedding a few months, with the Realm's gift still new. Spirited Away (6th) hasn't come yet.
  • Entertainer and Musician for the rabab; Lucky, because he married into the Hidden and came back.
