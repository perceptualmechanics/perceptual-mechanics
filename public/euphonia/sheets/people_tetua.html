The ʻarioi were a society devoted to the god ʻOro who traveled among the Society Islands performing dance, drama and satire, often mocking chiefs, and were fed lavishly for it.

A chief's performance ground in Tahiti, torches, the whole household watching. A young dancer of nineteen steps out and walks across the ground, and it is the chief's walk, exactly: the belly first, the pause to be admired, the glance to see who noticed. His own sons can't breathe for laughing. The chief laughs too, a moment late. Then he sends her a pig.

Background

A young ʻarioi of ʻOro's society from Raʻiātea, whose troupe mocks the chiefs in dance and verse and is fed handsomely for it. She has been in the troupe for two years and has already been asked to leave one island and begged to stay on another. The chiefs feed the ʻarioi lavishly because a chief who can't be laughed at is a chief people stop believing in. She would understand Ned Ashcombe perfectly, and would be much ruder. She wants to be the one who makes the Tuʻi Tonga's envoy laugh at himself, which no one in her troupe has managed.

How to play

  • She is comic relief who hears what chiefs say when they think the performers are only performing.
  • The Read and Coming and Going (not yet; 6th) are ahead; for now she is the dance and the joke.
  • In a fight she is Harlequin: fast, rude and hard to hit.
  • The hook is the Tuʻi Tonga's envoy. Whoever helps her make him laugh at himself will be in her repertoire forever.

Why this build

  • Why 4th: nineteen, two years in the troupe, one banishment.
  • College of Verse, as the roster has her: satire is her art.
  • Entertainer and Musician for the ʻarioi; Lucky, because chiefs who could have her killed send her pigs instead.
