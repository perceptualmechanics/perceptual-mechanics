Âşık Hüsniye

Human · Entertainer · Bard 4 (Lore) · level 4
PeopleThe Ottomans, Persia and the HornThe villages of Sivas and TokatCollege of LoreLevel 4

An âşık is a wandering minstrel-poet of Anatolia who sings to the saz in coffeehouses, at weddings and in village squares, improvising, dueling other âşıks in verse and singing the old romances. The favorite is Köroğlu, the bandit poet who rode against the beys and the pashas on his horse Kırat; the villages the pashas tax know every branch of it by heart (The Setting, Characters, Classes in 1668).

A village square outside Tokat at harvest, the threshing floors swept, the tax-farmer's men at the edge of the crowd. A woman with a saz across her knees sings the branch where Köroğlu comes down from Çamlıbel and takes back the miller's flour from the bey's steward, and every man in the square knows it and sings the refrain with her, louder each time, looking at the tax-farmer's men. The tax-farmer's men look at each other. She bows at the end, collects the coins, and is gone before they've finished deciding what to do.

Gear

Her master's saz, a knife (+4, 1d4 + 2), a felt cloak, a donkey called Kırat who is not a horse and does not care, a bag of coins in five currencies, a notebook of new verses in a script only she reads, a rosary of olive pits. Her master's saz. Mulberry wood, three courses of strings, the neck worn smooth by two lifetimes. The blind âşık left it to her and nobody else. A rival in Sivas offered her a horse for it. She sang about him instead.

The new branches. Seven new verses of Köroğlu about this year's tax-farmer, unnamed in the songs and recognized in every village. She has sung four of them. The other three are better.

Background

An âşık of thirty who walks the villages between Sivas and Tokat with a saz on her back, a donkey called Kırat after Köroğlu's horse, and a repertoire of every branch of the epic her master knew and two he didn't. She learned from an old blind âşık in Sivas, who taught her because she could rhyme him under the table at fourteen and who left her his saz. She duels other âşıks in the coffeehouses and wins more than she loses. She sings the bandit who stood up to the pashas in villages the pashas tax, and lately she has started making new branches about this year's tax-farmer, which the villages like better than the old ones. The tax-farmer has noticed. She wants to find the last branch of Köroğlu, the one her master said only one old man in Erzurum still sings, before the old man dies.

How to play

  • She is a contact who walks every village between Sivas and Tokat, and knows which tax-farmer is squeezing which valley, and why.
  • In a fight she makes the enemy forget its lines: Cutting Words, Vicious Mockery and a village singing the refrain.
  • Challenge her to a verse duel in a coffeehouse and lose gracefully, and she will be your friend in every village she sings in.
  • The hook is the last branch of Köroğlu. The old man in Erzurum is dying, and a party going east could take her, or carry it back.

Why this build

  • Why 4th: sixteen years with the saz, two since her master died; Cutting Words and a voice the villages answer.
  • College of Lore: Classes in 1668, Bard: "an âşık sings Köroğlu, the bandit who stood up to the pashas, in villages the pashas tax" (The Setting, Characters).
  • Entertainer and Skilled for a wandering singer; Alert, because she is always gone before the tax-farmer's men decide.
