Abdul Wahid

Human · Scribe · Counsellor 5 (Spymaster) · level 5
PeopleIndiaSuratSpymasterLevel 5

The muhtasib was the Mughal official who kept public morals and fair dealing in a city's markets. Aurangzeb's great law book, the Fatawa-i Alamgiri, was being compiled in the 1660s and 1670s; in Euphonia the empire licenses foreign magic, and someone has to read the permits.

Behind the customs house at Surat, a clerk's room with one good window. A thin young man in a white cap holds a Company wizard's permit up to the light, reads it twice, and asks the wizard, politely, after the health of the official who signed it. The wizard says he is well. The clerk says, still politely, that he died in the spring. Then he offers everyone tea.

The register of the dead. Every Mughal official who has signed a permit in ten years, with the date he left office or died. Forgers have tried to buy a copy. He has never sold one.

A permit nobody will name. Signed by a living official, and still wrong. He has not yet decided whom to show it to.

Background

The Surat muhtasib's clerk, who reads every foreigner's permit to practice magic and checks whether the official who signed it is still in office. He is honest, pious, underpaid and very good at his job, and he knows every seal, every hand and every death in the Mughal service from Agra to the coast. Smugglers fear him; Company wizards hate him; the muhtasib relies on him and forgets his name. He wants to be the first clerk in Surat to read the emperor's new law book, and find that it agrees with him.

How to play

  • He is a foil for a permit nobody will name (see Hooks), and the worst man a smuggler can meet.
  • He is honest. A party with a good permit has nothing to fear from him; a party with a bad one should not try to bribe him.
  • Dossiers and Network: he knows every seal and every official in the Mughal service, living or dead.
  • The hook is the permit nobody will name. He has found something, and he is deciding whether it is his job.

Why this build

  • Why 5th: a clerk, and the best reader of documents on the coast.
  • Spymaster: he doesn't spy; he reads, and that is worse.
  • Scribe and Skilled for the customs house; Alert for a man who notices.
