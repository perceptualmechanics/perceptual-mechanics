Hinerangi

PeopleOceaniaA coastal pā on Tīkapa Moana, AotearoaHunterLevel 6

Taniwha are guardians or dangers of particular rivers, lakes and seas, closer to dragons than fairies (The Setting, The State of the World, Oceania, The Hidden Here). Most are guardians, and a hapū that has one speaks to it and keeps its rules. The old stories also tell of taniwha that turned and took people, and of the warriors who went out against them with snares of flax and a tohunga's karakia.

A grey dawn on Tīkapa Moana, the tide on the turn. A woman wades out until the water reaches her chest, a coil of plaited flax over one shoulder and a carved taiaha held out of the water in her other hand. Two cousins wait in a canoe behind her with the end of the rope. She is watching one patch of sea that is too smooth, and she has been watching it since the stars went out.

The taiaha. Carved by her grandfather, the tongue at its head worked with the faces of the ancestors who used it before her. She talks to it before a hunt, and listens.

Background

A Māori hunter of the taniwha that turn to killing. Most taniwha are guardians, and her own harbor's has kept her family as long as there has been a family. When a hapū up or down the coast loses canoes and its tohunga decides the creature is no longer anyone's guardian, a gift comes to the pā on Tīkapa Moana and someone asks for Hinerangi. She comes with two cousins, her grandfather's taiaha and a rope as thick as her arm, asks for the karakia and the names of the people taken, and goes into the water. She has killed three. Each time, when the thing was opened, she stood with the hapū while it found what it was looking for, and a lament was sung. What she wants is never to be asked about her own harbor, whose taniwha has been seen too close to the canoes this summer.

How to play

  • She hunts big things. Colossus Slayer and the mark are both on every hit after the first, and Hunter's Lore tells her what the thing shrugs off before she wastes a swing.
  • Roving: she swims at her full Speed, which is the difference between standing in the water and fighting in it.
  • She won't hunt without a tohunga's word that the creature is lost to its people. Play that as the most important rule she keeps.
  • Out of the water she is a guest with good manners and a long memory for coastlines: Navigator's Tools and Survival with Expertise.
  • The hook is her own harbor's taniwha, seen too close to the canoes. If anyone has to decide whether it is still a guardian, she would rather it were someone else.

Why this build

  • Why 6th: three taniwha, and Roving, which puts her in the water at full Speed.
  • Hunter (SRD 5.2.1): the Pitch's "hunter fighting sea monsters in the South Pacific" (The Setting, Welcome to Euphonia, The Pitch). Colossus Slayer, because what she hunts is very large.
  • Sailor and Tavern Brawler for a canoe-house life; Tough for standing in cold water all day. A Strength ranger, because a taiaha is swung, not aimed.
